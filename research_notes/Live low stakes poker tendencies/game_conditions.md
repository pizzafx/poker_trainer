# Game conditions: live low-stakes NLHE cash games at US casinos ($1/$2, $1/$3, $2/$3, $2/$5, $3/$5)

Research date: 2026-11-10. Evidence tags used on every finding: **[DATA]** = posted schedule, observed/tracked sample, or first-party room page; **[SECONDARY]** = media/guide table compiled from room info, not independently verified; **[OPINION]** = coach/author estimate or "consensus" with no dataset; **[ANECDOTE]** = single player report/review. The date after the tag is the date of the information.

Method caveats that apply throughout:
- PokerAtlas listing pages and TwoPlusTwo threads returned HTTP 403 to direct fetches, so the two best primary sources for room-by-room rake/buy-ins and for tracked win-rate threads could not be read in full. Where a PokerAtlas or 2+2 figure appears below it comes from a search-result snippet and is flagged "snippet only".
- Bravo Poker Live, state regulator rake filings, Crush Live Poker, Hungry Horse (own site), Upswing and Red Chip win-rate articles were not reached directly. No published aggregate statistics from a bankroll-tracking app were found.
- Rake schedules change often; nothing here should be hard-coded without a per-room override.

## 1. Rake structures by room and region; which rooms spread $2/$3 and $3/$5

### Takeaway
Outside California and Texas, the standard structure is 10% of the pot in $1 increments, capped at $5-$7, "no flop no drop", plus a $1-$2 promo/jackpot drop at many rooms. Southern California uses a fixed per-hand drop (about $5-$7 plus $1 jackpot, largely independent of pot size) and is where $2/$3 and $3/$5 blinds actually exist; Texas clubs take no rake and charge about $12-$14/hour plus a $10 daily membership.

Comps $1-$2/hour (ARIA, Bellagio, Caesars, MGM Grand $2; Wynn and Resorts World $1.50; Venetian $1). [SECONDARY, published 2023-07-31, page last updated 2025-05-03] — [PokerNews, "Comparing Rake & Comps At Las Vegas Poker Rooms"](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2023/07/comparing-rake-across-las-vegas-poker-rooms-44211.htm)

**Northeast / other**
- Foxwoods: $1/$2 and $2/$5 NLH rake tops out at $6 per pot plus $1 to the bad beat jackpot. [SECONDARY, 2026, snippet only; a fandom wiki gives a different stepped schedule] — [Pokerology Foxwoods guide](https://www.pokerology.com/poker/features/foxwoods-poker-tournament-schedule/)
- General US picture: "nearly all rooms charge 10% up to at least $4"; newer rooms outside competitive areas 10% up to $5 or $6; bad beat/high hand/promo drops typically an extra $1 per hand, some $2. [OPINION/overview, 2017-08-07] — [PokerNews (Ashley Adams), "Let's Look at the Rake (and Time Charges)"](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/lets-look-at-the-rake-and-time-charges-28692.htm)
- General: typical live structure $5+$1 with the $1 promo drop taken once the pot reaches $10; highest the author has noted $6+$2. [OPINION/overview, undated, page copyright 2026] — [SplitSuit, "Live Poker Cash Game Truths"](https://www.splitsuit.com/live-poker-cash-game-truths)

### Inferences
- Rake cap in big blinds (arithmetic from the schedules above): $5 cap = 2.5bb at $1/$2, 1.67bb at $1/$3, 1.0bb at $2/$5; $6 cap = 2.0bb at $2/$3 and 1.2bb at $3/$5; $7 cap = 2.33bb at $1/$3 and 1.4bb at $2/$5. With 10% rake the cap is reached at a $50-$70 pot, i.e. 17-23bb at $1/$3 but only 10-14bb at $2/$5.
- A simulator needs at least three rake models: (a) percentage with cap and no-flop-no-drop plus threshold promo drop (Nevada, Northeast, most of the US); (b) fixed per-hand drop taken at low pot thresholds, with a small no-flop charge (Southern California, and reportedly Hard Rock Tampa); (c) time charge with zero pot rake (Texas).
- $2/$3 and $3/$5 are, on the evidence found, Southern California structures, so the most relevant rake model for those exact blinds is the fixed drop ($6 + $1 jackpot), not 10% capped.
- The Aria/Bellagio discrepancy ($5 in a December 2024 guide vs $7/$6 in a table updated May 2025) may reflect an increase between those dates or different games; it is unresolved.

### Gaps
- No current posted rake schedule was retrieved for Borgata, Mohegan Sun, Maryland Live, MGM National Harbor, Parx, bestbet Jacksonville, Hard Rock Hollywood, Gardens, Bicycle (post-2020), or any Midwest room. Only decade-old forum snippets surfaced (e.g. Maryland Live "10% up to $5 +$1 BBJ, no flop no drop", 2015), which are too old to use.
- Exact pot thresholds at which California rooms take each dollar of the fixed drop were not found.
- No state gaming regulator filing listing rake was found.
- No source enumerating every US room that spreads $2/$3 or $3/$5 was reachable (PokerAtlas blocked).

## 2. Total cost per hour / per 100 hands (rake + promo drop + tips), in dollars and big blinds

### Takeaway
The only observational study found puts rake at about $10-$12 per player-hour at Las Vegas $1/$2 (about 5bb/hour) and about $14/hour at $2/$5 (about 2.8bb/hour), at a fast 40 hands/hour; jackpot drop adds about $3/hour and tips a few dollars more. Rake is roughly twice as heavy in big-blind terms at the lowest stake as at $2/$5.

### Cited Findings
- Observed sample of "nearly 1,000 hands" of Las Vegas $1/$2 and $2/$5 across four cardrooms: 71% of hands were raked (reached a flop); average 8.78 players dealt in; about 40 hands/hour with an auto-shuffler; the average player wins 4.56 pots/hour. [DATA, small sample, 2019-02-06] — [PokerNews (Selbrede)](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)
- Average rake per hand dealt and per-player cost per hour from that sample: $1/$2 with $4 cap $2.16/hand, $9.86/hour; $1/$2 with $5 cap $2.46/hand, $11.22/hour; $1/$2 with $6 cap $2.72/hand, $12.40/hour; $2/$5 with $4 cap $2.92/hand, $13.99/hour. Average pot $50 at $1/$2 and $153 at $2/$5. [DATA for $4 cap; $5/$6 caps modelled on the same hands, 2019] — [PokerNews (Selbrede)](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)
- Each $1 added to the cap costs about $1.2-$1.4/hour per player at $1/$2 ($4 to $5: +$1.36/hour; $4 to $6: +$2.54/hour). [DATA-derived, 2019] — [PokerNews (Selbrede)](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)
- Average $1/$2 player loses "nearly $10/hour, about 5 BB/hour" to rake; average $2/$5 player about 2.8 BB/hour. [DATA-derived, 2019] — [PokerNews (Selbrede)](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)
- Jackpot drop of $1 per raked hand averages $0.71 per hand dealt, about $3.24/hour per player; total "taxes" for the average $1/$2 player about $13/hour with a $1 jackpot drop and about $16/hour with a $2 drop (most jackpot money is returned to players as promos). [DATA-derived, 2019] — [PokerNews (Selbrede)](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)
- Hypothetical 10-handed $1/$2 game at 30 hands/hour: rake averaging $4/hand ($120/hour), $1 bad-beat drop ($30/hour), $1 tip per pot ($30/hour) = $180/hour leaving the table, about $12/hour rake per player, $18/hour all-in. A $5 per half-hour time charge ($10/hour) would be about $2/hour cheaper than rake. [OPINION/illustrative model, 2017-08-07] — [PokerNews (Adams)](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/lets-look-at-the-rake-and-time-charges-28692.htm)
- Illustrative casino rake per player-hour: $1/$2 about $5-$8; $1/$3 about $6-$10; $2/$5 about $10-$14 (at 25-30 hands/hour), versus a flat ~$12/hour Texas seat fee. [OPINION, "illustrative averages", 2026] — [HoustonPoker](https://houstonpoker.com/seat-fee-math-houston)
- Texas all-in cost example: $12 seat + $2.50 membership share + $2 tips = about $16.50/hour before food; first hour $22 including daily membership. [OPINION/worked example, 2026] — [HoustonPoker](https://houstonpoker.com/seat-fee-math-houston)
- Tips: one tracking-app blog estimates dealer tips at $8-$12/hour ($1 per pot won, 25-30 hands/hour, "about one-third of pots won") and says net hourly runs 20-40% below gross after all expenses. [OPINION, 2026-10-17] — [PokerCharts, "How much do $1/$2 and $2/$5 players make"](https://pokercharts.com/en/blog/how-much-do-1-2-and-2-5-poker-players-make); CONTRADICTED on the tip arithmetic by Selbrede's observed 4.56 pots won per player-hour (which implies about $4.50/hour at $1 per pot) — [PokerNews (Selbrede)](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm) — and by HoustonPoker's "$2/hour" — [HoustonPoker](https://houstonpoker.com/seat-fee-math-houston)
- Canadian contrast (not US): Ontario rooms rake 10% up to $15-$20 at $1/$3. Useful only as an upper bound. [SECONDARY, 2025-09-02] — [PokerNews (Sofen)](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2025/09/expensive-poker-cash-game-rake-in-canada-49588.htm)

### Inferences
- Per 100 hands per player (Selbrede figures divided by 8.78 players): $1/$2 with $5 cap about $28/100 hands = 14bb/100; $2/$5 with $4 cap about $33-$35/100 hands = about 7bb/100. These are the pool-average rake burden; a tight winning player pays less than average in absolute terms but the figure is the right order of magnitude for calibration.
- Rescaling Selbrede's 40 hands/hour to the more commonly cited 30 hands/hour cuts hourly rake by a quarter: about $8.40/hour at $1/$2 with a $5 cap (4.2bb/hour) and about $10.50/hour at $2/$5 with a $4 cap (2.1bb/hour). No direct measurement exists for $1/$3 or for $2/$5 with a $5-$7 cap; interpolating, $1/$3 with a $5 cap should sit at roughly $9-$12/hour (3-4bb/hour), which should be treated as an estimate.
- Texas seat fee at $13/hour = 4.3bb/hour at $1/$3 and 2.6bb/hour at $2/$5, similar to capped-rake burdens but independent of how many pots a player enters (so it does not distort pot odds in-hand).
- Southern California fixed drop: at $6+$1 on every flop, 30 hands/hour, about 71% of hands seeing a flop and 9 players, the per-player cost is about $16.50/hour (about 5.5bb/hour at $2/$3, 3.3bb/hour at $3/$5). All four inputs are assumptions carried over from other sources; no LA-specific measurement was found.
- Dealer tips at $1 per pot won are realistically about $3-$5/hour for an average-activity player (pots won per hour is about hands per hour divided by players), not $8-$12.

### Gaps
- No measured rake-per-hour study after 2019, none for $1/$3, $2/$3 or $3/$5, and none for California fixed-drop games.
- No data on what fraction of jackpot/promo drop is returned to a typical regular (high-hand and bad-beat equity).
- Tipping behaviour is opinion only; no measured average tip per pot.

## 3. Effect of rake on strategy

### Takeaway
Solver work shows rake mainly destroys the value of flat-calling preflop: cold-calling ranges shrink 25-35% and some calls become 3-bets, big-blind defence shrinks sharply, and small pots are taxed at the full 10%. No solver study was found that uses an actual live structure (10% capped at $5-$7 on $1/$3 blinds, or a fixed California drop).

### Cited Findings
- 200bb 6-max solves, raked ("100NL rake", no flop no drop) vs unraked: facing a LJ raise, HJ, CO and BTN VPIP 25-35% fewer hands in the raked game; calling drops most and some hands shift to 3-bets; the BB calls a min-raise in the raked game "barely 60% as often" as unraked; in the raked game raise size barely changes in-position VPIP. Results at 100bb were "not much different". [DATA, solver output, 2024-03-19] — [GTO Wizard blog (Andrew Brokos), "Preflop Raise Sizing: Examining 2 Key Factors"](https://blog.gtowizard.com/preflop-raise-sizing-examining-2-key-factors/)
- Same study, open-size EVs under rake: LJ AA 10.15bb at a 3bb open vs 9.48bb at 2bb; LJ QQ 1.13bb at 2bb vs 0.95bb at 3bb; on the BTN, weak opens (J4s, T5s, K7o, A2o) have higher EV at 3bb only in the raked game because of no-flop-no-drop. [DATA, solver output, 2024-03-19] — [GTO Wizard blog](https://blog.gtowizard.com/preflop-raise-sizing-examining-2-key-factors/)
- With no ante and a rake, the big blind facing a cutoff raise can defend only about 29% of hands, versus 76% in an unraked tournament with a big-blind ante; ranges should tighten further as rake becomes a larger share of the pot. [OPINION citing solver-derived ranges, date not captured, snippet only] — [Card Player, "More Rake Is Not Better For A Wider Range"](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/1632969-acr-poker-39-1/articles/1633660-more-rake-is-not-better-for-a-wider-range)
- A $5 cap at $1/$2 equals 2.5 big blinds per capped pot (attributed to Jonathan Little). [OPINION/arithmetic, cited 2026-08-05] — [TableLab, "What's a Good Win Rate in Live Poker?"](https://tablelab.app/blog/good-live-poker-win-rate)
- Worked small-pot example: a $34 pot taken down by a $20 bet pays $3 rake + $1 drop, so the winner receives $30, an 11.8% tax on the pot. [OPINION/arithmetic, undated, copyright 2026] — [SplitSuit](https://www.splitsuit.com/live-poker-cash-game-truths)
- Rake share of gross winnings in illustrative $1/$2 player types: a $20/hour net winner pays about 33% of pre-rake profit in rake; a $5/hour winner about 67%; a break-even player 100%. At $2/$5: $40/hour winner about 26%, $10/hour winner about 58%. [OPINION built on the observed rake figures, 2019] — [PokerNews (Selbrede)](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)
- GTO Wizard offers customizable raked solutions; a custom-rake postflop example found the BB calling less often in all spots as rake rises, with those calls shifting to folds and check-raises. [DATA, solver output, date not captured, snippet only] — [GTO Wizard blog, "Customizable Raked Solutions With GTO Wizard AI"](https://blog.gtowizard.com/customizable-raked-solutions-with-gto-wizard-ai/)

### Inferences
- Under 10%-capped rake, every pot below the cap threshold ($50-$70) is taxed at the full 10% (plus promo drop), so limped pots, single-raised heads-up pots and blind-versus-blind pots are the most rake-distorted spots; 3-bet and deep-stacked pots hit the cap and pay a much smaller percentage. This follows directly from the schedules in section 1 and matches the direction of the solver results.
- Under a California fixed drop ($6+$1), a 4-way limped $2/$3 pot of $12 would lose more than half its value to the drop if the full drop were taken at that size; the true tax depends on the unknown drop thresholds, but small-pot play is clearly far more penalised than under percentage rake. Flag as needing verification before encoding.
- "No flop no drop" creates a discrete EV step at the flop: preflop fold equity is worth more than a chip-EV calculation suggests, favouring 3-bet-or-fold over flatting. The simulator's after-rake EV scoring should apply rake only on flop-seen branches (except in fixed-drop rooms with a no-flop charge).
- The solver studies found use online structures at 6-max and 100-200bb. Live games are 8-9 handed with larger opens and multiway pots, so the published percentages (25-35% fewer calls, 60% BB call frequency) are directional, not transferable constants.

### Gaps
- No published solver analysis using a live low-stakes structure (cap of 1-2.5bb at 9-handed, or fixed drop), and none quantifying which specific hands flip from +EV to -EV in blind defence under live rake.
- No quantified analysis of rake impact on limped or multiway pots specifically.

## 4. Buy-in rules by stake and room/region

### Takeaway
Buy-in caps vary by a factor of ten in big blinds across regions: Southern California's small games are capped at 13-100bb, Las Vegas $1/$3 at 100-167bb and $2/$5 at 240-300bb, and Texas clubs at 333-400bb. Typical minimums are $100 at $1/$3 and $200-$300 at $2/$5.

### Cited Findings
- Las Vegas (per room guide): Wynn $1/$3 $100-$500, $2/$5 max $1,500; Aria $100-$500 (stake not split); Bellagio $1/$3 $100-$300; Venetian $1/$3 $100-$300; Caesars $1/$3 $100-$300; Horseshoe $1/$3 $100-$300; Resorts World $1/$3 $100-$400, $2/$5 max $1,200 (room since closed); South Point $1/$2 $100-$300; Orleans $100-$500; Golden Nugget $1/$2 $100 minimum, no maximum. [SECONDARY, 2024-12-05] — [PokerCoaching.com](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/best-poker-rooms-in-las-vegastop-10-picks/)
- Southern California: Commerce $1/$2 $40 (min = max), $2/$3 $100 fixed; Bicycle $2/$3 $100-$300, $5/$5 cap $1,000; Gardens $1/$2 $40, $2/$3 $100-$200, $5/$5 $300-$600; Hollywood Park $1/$2 $40-$100, $3/$5 $100-$300; Hustler $1/$3 $40-$100, $5/$5 $300-$1,000; Pechanga $1/$2 and $1/$3 $40-$100, $2/$3 $100-$300. [SECONDARY, 2020-01-05/2020-06-13] — [Red Chip Poker](https://redchippoker.com/los-angeles-best-poker-games/)

- Connecticut, Foxwoods: $1/$3 $100-$500; $2/$5 $300-$1,000. [SECONDARY, 2026, snippet only] — [Pokerology Foxwoods guide](https://www.pokerology.com/poker/features/foxwoods-poker-tournament-schedule/)
- A 2024 challenge was played at Hustler $5/$5 with a $1,000 buy-in cap (200bb). [ANECDOTE consistent with listings, 2024] — [Pokerati](https://pokerati.com/2025/01/is-live-poker-still-beatable-in-2025-here-is-the-truth/)
- General: live minimum buy-in is usually 50-80bb and maximum usually 100-250bb; some $1/$2 games allow up to 300bb. [SECONDARY/encyclopedic, undated] — [Wikipedia, "Buying in (poker)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buying_in_(poker))
- Match-the-stack (buying in for the largest stack at the table) exists as a room option and is described by The Lodge; it was removed there in 2022. [DATA, first-party] — [The Lodge, "What Is Match the Stack Poker?"](https://thelodgepokerclub.com/what-is-match-the-stack-poker/)

### Inferences
- Maximum buy-in in big blinds, computed from the above: Commerce $1/$2 20bb; Hustler $1/$3 13-33bb; Commerce $2/$3 33bb; Gardens $2/$3 33-67bb; Bicycle/Pechanga $2/$3 33-100bb; Hollywood Park $3/$5 20-60bb; Bellagio/Venetian/Caesars $1/$3 33-100bb; Wynn/Aria $1/$3 33-167bb; Turning Stone $2/$5 40-100bb; Foxwoods $2/$5 60-200bb; Wynn $2/$5 up to 300bb; Lodge $1/$3 67-333bb; Lodge $2/$5 40-400bb.
- The "typical" presets the requester suggested ($100-$300 at $1/$3; $300-$500 or $1,000 at $2/$5) are supported for Las Vegas and the Northeast, but not for Southern California (much shallower at the $2/$3 and $3/$5 blinds) or Texas (much deeper). Buy-in range should be a per-room parameter.
- For the exact $2/$3 and $3/$5 blinds, the best-sourced buy-in ranges are $100 fixed to $100-$300 at $2/$3 and $100-$300 at $3/$5 (2020 data), implying starting stacks of 33-100bb and 20-60bb respectively.

### Gaps
- No current buy-in listings retrieved for Borgata, Mohegan Sun, Maryland Live, MGM National Harbor, Parx, Hard Rock Hollywood/Tampa, bestbet, or Midwest rooms.
- Southern California buy-ins are from 2020 except Hustler; current $2/$3 and $3/$5 caps at Commerce, Bicycle and Gardens are unverified.
- No list of which rooms currently use match-the-stack or uncapped small games beyond Golden Nugget (no max at $1/$2) and the Lodge's removed option.

## 5. Real stack-depth distribution and effective stacks

### Takeaway
No measured distribution of live stack depths was found; this is the largest gap. What exists is structural (buy-in caps, section 4) plus conflicting anecdotes that typical $1/$3 stacks are either $100-$300 or $300-$500.

### Cited Findings
- One guide says live $1/$3 games often sit with $300-$500 stacks (100-166bb); another says the average stack in such games is usually $100-$300 (33-100bb). [OPINION, conflicting, undated, snippets only] — [Calculated Poker Substack, "How to Crush $1/$3 and $2/$5 Live Poker"](https://calculatedpoker.substack.com/p/how-to-crush-13-and-25-live-poker); [RiverOdds, open-raise sizing guide](https://riverodds.app/poker-open-raise-sizing/) (attribution between these two is uncertain; both came from one search summary)
- Live stacks are described as "quite shallow", with no numbers. [OPINION, undated, copyright 2026] — [SplitSuit](https://www.splitsuit.com/live-poker-cash-game-truths)
- Average pot observed in Las Vegas: about $50 at $1/$2 (25bb) and $153 at $2/$5 (about 31bb). [DATA, small sample, 2019] — [PokerNews (Selbrede)](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)
- Buy-in caps bound starting stacks at 13-100bb in Southern California small games, 100-167bb at Las Vegas $1/$3, up to 333-400bb in Texas. [see section 4 sources] — [Red Chip Poker](https://redchippoker.com/los-angeles-best-poker-games/); [PokerCoaching.com](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/best-poker-rooms-in-las-vegastop-10-picks/); [The Lodge](https://thelodgepokerclub.com/changing-cash-games/)
- Golden Nugget's $1/$2 with no maximum is described as playing deep-stacked. [SECONDARY, 2024-12-05] — [PokerCoaching.com](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/best-poker-rooms-in-las-vegastop-10-picks/)

### Inferences
- Because no distribution data exists, the simulator's stack-depth distribution should be an explicit, configurable assumption anchored to the room's buy-in min/max, not presented as empirical. A defensible construction is: starting stacks spread between the room minimum and maximum with a mass at the maximum, then widened to represent session drift. Any specific percentages (e.g. share of players under 50bb) would be invented and are deliberately not given here.
- Effective stacks in capped Southern California games must frequently be under 40bb at $1/$2, $1/$3 and $2/$3 simply because the cap is 20-33bb at several rooms, which makes those games structurally different (closer to short-stack play) from Las Vegas or Texas games at nominally similar stakes.

### Gaps
- No source with counted or sampled stack sizes at a live table at any stake; no data on how stacks grow through a session, on rebuy/top-up behaviour, or on the share of short, 100bb and deep stacks.
- TwoPlusTwo LLSNL threads that may contain player-collected observations were not reachable (403).

## 7. Straddle rules and frequency

### Takeaway
Straddle rules are set room by room; UTG is the most common form, button and Mississippi straddles are described as common in the southern US. No room-by-room or frequency data was found.

### Cited Findings
- Some rooms allow only UTG straddles, some allow Mississippi and button straddles, some forbid straddles; the UTG straddle is the most common variant. [OPINION/overview, undated, snippet only] — [PokerCoaching.com, "Poker Straddles"](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/poker-straddles/)
- "House rules permitting Mississippi straddles are common in the southern United States"; with a button/Mississippi straddle the small blind acts first preflop and the straddler last. [SECONDARY, undated, snippet only] — [PokerChipForum thread quoting Wikipedia](https://www.pokerchipforum.com/threads/straddle-rule-in-cash-games.112277/); [PokerSkill glossary](https://www.pokerskill.com/poker-glossary/straddle/)
- At Hustler $5/$5 in 2024, straddles and side games (the "2-7 game", "nit game") were described as frequent, with no frequencies. [ANECDOTE, 2024] — [Pokerati](https://pokerati.com/2025/01/is-live-poker-still-beatable-in-2025-here-is-the-truth/)

### Inferences
- Straddling should be a per-room rule set (none / UTG only / button / any seat) plus a per-table frequency parameter; no source supports a default frequency.

### Gaps
- No straddle policy found for any named room (Las Vegas, California, Florida, Texas, Northeast); no data on how often hands are straddled at any stake.

## 8. Differences between stake levels

### Takeaway
Quantified differences are almost entirely economic (rake burden in bb, buy-in depth); differences in player mix, aggression and 3-bet frequency are asserted only qualitatively and inconsistently.

### Cited Findings
- Rake burden: about 5bb/hour for the average $1/$2 player vs 2.8bb/hour at $2/$5; average $2/$5 rake per hand about one-third higher in dollars than $1/$2. [DATA, 2019] — [PokerNews (Selbrede)](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)
- Average pot about 25bb at $1/$2 vs about 31bb at $2/$5. [DATA, small sample, 2019] — [PokerNews (Selbrede)](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)
- Coach-style win-rate ceilings compress in bb terms as stakes rise: 10bb/hour at $1/$2, 8bb/hour at $2/$5, 5bb/hour at $5/$10. [OPINION, undated, copyright 2026] — [SplitSuit](https://www.splitsuit.com/live-poker-cash-game-truths)
- Forum views (snippets): one poster found no change in the pool between $1/$3 and $2/$5; another said the main difference is the occasional professional at the higher stake; another that $1/$2 players call large 3-bets that $2/$5 players fold to. [ANECDOTE, undated, snippets only, thread not fetched] — [TwoPlusTwo, "$1/2 vs $1/3 - $2/5 vs $5/5 what's the diff?"](https://forumserver.twoplustwo.com/170/live-no-limit-holdem-cash/1-2-vs-1-3-2-5-vs-5-5-whats-diff-1378241/)
- Open sizes: live standard described as 3-4bb plus 1bb per limper; one $2/$5 player opens $20 (4bb) "almost every time"; a $1/$2 game with a $15 (7.5bb) standard open is reported at one casino. [OPINION/ANECDOTE, undated, snippets only] — [RiverOdds](https://riverodds.app/poker-open-raise-sizing/); [TwoPlusTwo beginner forum thread](https://forumserver.twoplustwo.com/32/beginners-general-questions/open-raise-size-cash-games-vs-live-games-1624087/)
- 3-betting is "rare at these tables" ($1/$3 and $2/$5). [OPINION, undated, snippet only] — [Calculated Poker Substack](https://calculatedpoker.substack.com/p/how-to-crush-13-and-25-live-poker)
- Room character differs within a stake: Aria "many pros, can feel hostile to recreational players"; Caesars "softest games, heavy tourist traffic"; South Point "local regulars". [OPINION, 2024-12-05] — [PokerCoaching.com](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/best-poker-rooms-in-las-vegastop-10-picks/)

### Inferences
- $2/$3 and $3/$5, being California blinds with shallow caps and fixed drops, differ from $1/$3 and $2/$5 elsewhere more by structure (stack depth and rake form) than by the nominal blind size; treating "$2/$3 is like $1/$3" and "$3/$5 is like $2/$5" is reasonable for player tendencies only if stack depth and rake are set from the California parameters.

### Gaps
- No data on proportion of regulars vs recreational players, VPIP/PFR/3-bet frequencies, or open sizes by stake. (Population-tendency data is presumably covered by other researchers; nothing quantified surfaced here.)

## 9. Achievable win rates, variance, and share of winners

### Takeaway
There is no public large-sample tracked dataset of live win rates; every "benchmark" is opinion or a small self-reported sample. The opinions converge on about 5bb/hour as a good winner and 8-10bb/hour as the ceiling for the best players at $1/$2-$2/$5 (about 17-33bb/100 at 30 hands/hour), with individual reports up to 18bb/hour over a few hundred hours.

### Cited Findings

**Benchmarks (opinion)**
- Good 5bb/hour, strong 7-8bb/hour, elite 10+bb/hour at both $1/$2 ($10 / $14-$16 / $20+ per hour) and $2/$5 ($25 / $35-$40 / $50+ per hour); $5/$10 good 4-5, strong 6-8, elite 10-15bb/hour. Explicitly described as "industry consensus across forums, coaching communities and long-running player pools", not the app's own user data; tables assume 500+ hours. [OPINION, 2026-08-22] — [PokerCharts, "Good Win Rate in Live Poker"](https://pokercharts.com/en/blog/win-rate-benchmarks-live-poker)
- "Top-side" estimates: $20/hour at $1/$2 (10bb), $40/hour at $2/$5 (8bb), $50/hour at $5/$10 (5bb); a solid $1/$2 winner $10-$20/hour. [OPINION, undated, copyright 2026] — [SplitSuit](https://www.splitsuit.com/live-poker-cash-game-truths)
- "Very best" players: about 10bb/hour at $1/$2 ($20) and $2/$5 ($50), 9bb/hour at $5/$10; the citing author calls these figures "wild speculations"; an older rule of thumb was 1bb/hour; Red Chip is cited for a full-time $1/$2 ceiling of about $25/hour. [OPINION, cited 2026-08-05] — [TableLab](https://tablelab.app/blog/good-live-poker-win-rate) summarising [BlackRain79, "What is a Good Poker Hourly Rate?"](https://www.blackrain79.com/2018/06/good-poker-hourly-rate.html)
- $1/$2 solid winners 5-10bb/hour, exceptional 12-15bb/hour; $2/$5 regulars 6-10bb/hour ($30-$50/hour). [OPINION, 2026-10-17; note this is more generous than the same site's benchmark page] — [PokerCharts, "How much do $1/$2 and $2/$5 players make"](https://pokercharts.com/en/blog/how-much-do-1-2-and-2-5-poker-players-make)
- Illustrative player types: strong $1/$2 winner $20/hour net (about $30/hour before rake); solid $5/hour; at $2/$5 strong $40/hour, solid $10/hour. [OPINION, 2019] — [PokerNews (Selbrede)](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)
- Dissent: skeptical commenters hold that 10bb/hour sustained at live $2/$5 is "unheard of". [OPINION, undated, snippet only; exact page not verified] — [BlackRain79](https://www.blackrain79.com/2018/06/good-poker-hourly-rate.html)

**Self-reported / tracked results (small samples)**
- Las Vegas $2/$5 professional: "$60 an hour at $2-$5 for a long time", later about $50/hour (12 then 10bb/hour), decline attributed to tougher games and less volume/study. [ANECDOTE/self-report, sample size not stated, 2019-03] — [Card Player (Ryan Laplante), "Being A Poker Pro: Part 2"](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66406-ocean-s-eleven-casino-32-7/articles/23641-poker-fundamentals-being-a-poker-pro-part-2)
- Coach Marc Goone (Hungry Horse Poker): 300 hours of $5/$5 ($1,000 cap) at Hustler, Los Angeles, in 2024: $89.61/hour before jackpot payouts, about 18bb/hour; about $110/hour including rakeback by the article's estimate. The article itself calls 300 hours "not conclusive". [DATA, tracked single-player sample, small, 2024; published 2025-01-03] — [Pokerati](https://pokerati.com/2025/01/is-live-poker-still-beatable-in-2025-here-is-the-truth/)
- One blogger's personal tracked sample: 2,735 hours at about $83 CAD/hour with a ±$25/hour band (stake not captured in the extract; Canadian games); hourly fell with session length (under 5h $141; 5-7h $117; 7-8h $76; over 8h $23). [DATA, single-player, 2026-08-05] — [TableLab](https://tablelab.app/blog/good-live-poker-win-rate)

**Variance and sample size**
- Live full-ring NLHE standard deviation "commonly cited" as 80-100bb/100 hands; no dataset. [OPINION, undated, copyright 2026] — [PokerCharts, "How Many Hours Until You Can Trust Your Win Rate?"](https://pokercharts.com/en/blog/how-many-hours-until-you-can-trust-your-win-rate)
- 90bb/100 used as an assumed SD; at that SD 100,000 hands gives about ±5.6bb/100 at 95% confidence. [OPINION/arithmetic, 2026-08-05] — [TableLab](https://tablelab.app/blog/good-live-poker-win-rate)
- Example confidence interval: 200 hours at $1/$2 showing $20/hour has a 95% interval of roughly -$5 to +$45/hour; a 5bb/hour winner may face a 300bb downswing over 100 hours; 300-500 hours minimum before a live rate starts to stabilise; 1,000+ hours for a reasonably tight estimate. [OPINION/arithmetic, 2026] — [PokerCharts benchmarks](https://pokercharts.com/en/blog/win-rate-benchmarks-live-poker); [PokerCharts sample-size post](https://pokercharts.com/en/blog/how-many-hours-until-you-can-trust-your-win-rate)
- Bankroll guidance 20-30 buy-ins; downswings of 20-30 buy-ins cited as possible. [OPINION, 2026] — [SplitSuit](https://www.splitsuit.com/live-poker-cash-game-truths); [PokerCharts](https://pokercharts.com/en/blog/how-much-do-1-2-and-2-5-poker-players-make)

**Share of long-term winners**
- "Roughly 95% of players lose over the long run" (no dataset). [OPINION, 2026-08-22] — [PokerCharts benchmarks](https://pokercharts.com/en/blog/win-rate-benchmarks-live-poker)
- About 30% of players are long-term winners; a database sample of 609 players with 10,000+ hands found about 30% profitable after rake (online-derived; live not separated). [OPINION plus online DATA, undated, snippets only; which of the two pages holds the 609-player sample was not verified] — [BlackRain79, "What Percentage of Poker Players Are Winners?"](https://www.blackrain79.com/2019/09/what-percentage-of-poker-players-are.html); [Automatic Poker](https://automaticpoker.com/lifestyle/what-percentage-of-poker-players-win/)
- "Breaking even places a player in the top 50%." [OPINION, undated] — [SplitSuit](https://www.splitsuit.com/live-poker-cash-game-truths)
- Rake and jackpot drops are "the main reason few low-stakes players win"; a player must be significantly better than average to overcome them. [OPINION grounded in the rake data, 2019] — [PokerNews (Selbrede)](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)

### Inferences
- Conversions at 30 hands/hour: 5bb/hour = 16.7bb/100; 8bb/hour = 26.7bb/100; 10bb/hour = 33.3bb/100. At 25 hands/hour the same hourly rates are 20, 32 and 40bb/100. Dollar equivalents at the target blinds: 5-10bb/hour is $15-$30/hour at $2/$3 and $25-$50/hour at $3/$5.
- These after-rake rates sit on top of a pool-average rake burden of roughly 7-14bb/100 (section 2), so a "good" winner's pre-rake edge over the field is very large by online standards; that is consistent with the claim that live fields are weak, but it is not independent evidence.
- Hourly standard deviation implied by 80-100bb/100 at 30 hands/hour is about 44-55bb/hour (SD per 100 × sqrt(0.30)). With a 5bb/hour true win rate and 50bb/hour SD, the standard error after 500 hours is about 2.2bb/hour, so a 95% interval is roughly 0.6-9.4bb/hour; this is why small self-reports such as 18bb/hour over 300 hours cannot distinguish "elite" from "good plus variance".
- A defensible benchmark for the simulator's "good player" is about 5bb/hour after rake before tips, with 8-10bb/hour as "excellent" and anything above that flagged as unverified; all tiers should be labelled opinion-based.
- The share-of-winners figures (5% to 30%) are irreconcilable and none is live-measured; do not present any as fact.

### Gaps
- No large-sample tracked live results by stake (no published aggregate from any tracking app; TwoPlusTwo win-rate threads unreachable).
- No measured live standard deviation per hour or per 100 hands. A figure of 120-160bb/100 for live $1/$2-$2/$5 appeared in one search summary but could not be traced to a specific page, so it is not cited.
- Bart Hanson/Crush Live Poker, Hungry Horse's own published figures, Red Chip and Upswing win-rate articles were not retrieved directly.
- No win-rate figures specific to $2/$3 or $3/$5 blinds, and none that state clearly whether tips are deducted.

## 10. Trends 2022-2026

### Takeaway
Rake caps have crept up (a $4 cap was the Las Vegas base case in 2019; $5 is now the floor and $6-$7 appears at flagship rooms), and coaches consistently say games are tougher than in the late 2010s but still beatable. None of the "tougher" claims is backed by data.

### Cited Findings
- Las Vegas rake: $4 cap as the modelled base case in 2019; by 2023-2025 most rooms $5, Bellagio and Boulder Station $6, ARIA $7. [DATA 2019 vs SECONDARY 2023-2025] — [PokerNews (Selbrede) 2019](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm); [PokerNews rake table, updated 2025-05-03](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2023/07/comparing-rake-across-las-vegas-poker-rooms-44211.htm)
- Hustler $1/$3: $5+$1 in 2020, about $5 cap + $1 no-flop charge around 2022, $7 on the current PokerAtlas listing. [SECONDARY, three dates; last is snippet only] — [Red Chip 2020](https://redchippoker.com/los-angeles-best-poker-games/); [PokerNews Hustler page](https://www.pokernews.com/poker-rooms/hustler-casino.htm); [PokerAtlas](https://www.pokeratlas.com/poker-cash-game/hustler-casino-gardena-no-limit-holdem-1-3)
- Commerce stopped comping players as of 2021. [SECONDARY, 2022-09] — [PokerNews Commerce page](https://www.pokernews.com/poker-rooms/commerce-casino.htm)
- Room closures/disruptions: Resorts World Las Vegas poker room closed (after December 2024); The Lodge (Texas) raided March 2026 and reopened May 2026. — [PokerCoaching.com](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/best-poker-rooms-in-las-vegastop-10-picks/); [Pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/live-poker/united-states/poker-in-texas/)
- Texas buy-in caps were raised in 2022 (Lodge $1/$3 to $1,000, $2/$5 to $2,000), i.e. deeper games. [DATA, 2022-05-05] — [The Lodge](https://thelodgepokerclub.com/changing-cash-games/)
- Games "harder than in 2017 but still good"; opponents still call too much preflop and under-4-bet; side games and straddles keep games soft. [OPINION from a coach with a 300-hour 2024 sample] — [Pokerati](https://pokerati.com/2025/01/is-live-poker-still-beatable-in-2025-here-is-the-truth/)
- A Las Vegas $2/$5 pro's hourly fell from $60 to about $50 by 2019, attributed partly to tougher games. [ANECDOTE, 2019] — [Card Player (Laplante)](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66406-ocean-s-eleven-casino-32-7/articles/23641-poker-fundamentals-being-a-poker-pro-part-2)
- Solver and AI-trainer study is said to be spreading to low-stakes players, with tighter and more standardised preflop play. [OPINION, 2025, snippet only] — [WPT Global blog, "How Tech and AI Shaped Poker in 2025"](https://wptglobal.com/poker/blog/news/poker-tech-ai-2025)
- Live $1/$2 still described as softer than online micro-stakes. [OPINION, 2026] — [SplitSuit](https://www.splitsuit.com/live-poker-cash-game-truths)

### Inferences
- The direction of travel on rake is up by about $1-$3 per pot at the cap since 2019; using Selbrede's sensitivity (about $1.2-$1.4 per player-hour per $1 of cap at $1/$2, at 40 hands/hour), a move from a $4 to a $6-$7 cap costs the average $1/$2-$1/$3 player roughly an extra $2.50-$4/hour. This is an extrapolation beyond the modelled $6 cap.
- Win-rate benchmarks published before about 2020 (the "10bb/hour" folklore) were formed under lower rake and reportedly softer fields, so they should be discounted rather than used as current targets.

### Gaps
- No data on player-pool size or composition changes 2022-2026 (post-pandemic traffic, regular-to-recreational ratio).
- No dated record of when specific rooms raised rake (e.g. when ARIA went to $7 or Bellagio to $6).
- No trend data for Florida, Northeast or Midwest rooms.
