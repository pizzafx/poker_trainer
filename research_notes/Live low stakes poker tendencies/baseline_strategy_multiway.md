## 1. Baseline preflop strategy (ranges, open sizing, limping, 3-bets, blind defence and rake, straddles, stack depth)

### Takeaway
Consensus is "tight, raise-or-fold, bigger sizes than online": open to roughly 4-6bb at $1/$2 (4bb + 1bb per limper is the modal formula), play mostly pairs, suited broadways, suited connectors and suited aces, 3-bet a linear value-heavy range, and cut calling ranges (especially blind defence) because of rake and large open sizes. Concrete live full-ring opening charts by position were NOT found in any accessible source; over-limping is a genuine disagreement.

### Cited Findings

**Hand selection / ranges**
- Ed Miller's Skill #1 for $1-$2: play mostly pocket pairs, suited big cards and suited connectors; avoid ragged suited hands and most offsuit hands; raise frequently; "never limp from any position outside the blinds"; reraise often (he describes himself as usually the tightest player and the most frequent 3-bettor); use one static preflop strategy rather than adjusting. No percentages given. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Ed Miller, "Three Skills to Beat $1-$2", Card Player 28/2](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66294-cppt-foxwoods-28-2/articles/22227-three-skills-to-beat-1-2)
- Miller: "Nearly everyone in a live $2-$5 game plays way too many hands preflop", which is the root of their later-street problems. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Ed Miller, "3 Ways $2-$5 Regs Shoot Themselves In The Foot", Red Chip Poker](https://redchippoker.com/how-live-regs-shoot-themselves-in-the-foot/amp/)
- Las Vegas $1/$2 population baseline: average player is about 35% VPIP / 6% PFR; limping range modelled as roughly the 6.1%-35% band of hands; a limper calls a $10 raise about 56% of the time; the button calls a cutoff raise about 22%; blinds raise a limped pot only 4.8% (SB) and 3.4% (BB), so a button over-limp behind one limper produces a four-way pot about 92% of the time. [DATA][LIVE] (sample size not stated; method referenced to the author's book "Donkey Poker Vol. 2") — [Steve Selbrede, "Preflop Raising in Live $1/$2 No-Limit Hold'em Cash Games", PokerNews 2017](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/preflop-raising-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-cash-games-29209.htm)
- Winning TAGs in a live database (242 players, ~500k hands, $2/$5 to $100/$200) average 31 VPIP / 20 PFR with a 13% 3-bet (vs ~8-10% online). Players-to-flop distribution: heads-up 45%, 3-way 24%, 4+ way 31%. [DATA][LIVE, but higher stakes, mostly 500-1000bb deep, 85% of the $2/$5-$5/$10 games straddled; TAG-filtered; rake ignored] — [Hand2Note blog, "Online vs live poker insights"](https://hand2note4-com-test-dzf5dpdueud7cqdm.westeurope-01.azurewebsites.net/Blog/Databases/online-vs-live-poker-insights)
- Generic full-ring (not live-specific) percentages seen only in snippets: UTG 9-handed ~9-12% (other pages say 12-16%), EP 12-15%, MP 18-22%, CO 26-32%, BTN 40-50%. [OPINION][not live-specific](snippet; SEO-style sites, low confidence) — [DeucesCracked EP ranges](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-early-position-opening-ranges-utg-6max-full-ring-2026); [PokerSkill opening charts](https://www.pokerskill.com/charts/opening-ranges/)

**Open-raise sizing**
- Rule of thumb in the one data-based article: about 4x BB plus 1 BB per limper ($10, $12, $14, $16 at $1/$2). [OPINION built on DATA][LIVE] — [Selbrede, PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/preflop-raising-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-cash-games-29209.htm)
- Upswing: live iso/open formula 4bb + 1bb per limper (online 3bb + 1); if loose players keep calling, step up 1bb at a time (6bb, 7bb...) "until those players start folding", then revert one step. On splashy tables: if you normally open 3bb go to 4bb, and keep adding 1bb "until you find that you are playing many more heads-up pots"; splashy-game averages reach "upwards of 5bb". [OPINION][LIVE] — [Upswing, "How to Maximize Your Winnings Versus Multiple Limpers"](https://upswingpoker.com/vs-multiple-limpers/); [Upswing, "How to Adjust on Loose & Splashy Tables"](https://upswingpoker.com/splashy-live-games/)
- Upswing podcast quiz examples: $7-$10 opens at $1/$2 with $300 stacks, $12-$14 with a strong hand at a loose table; in soft live games varying size by hand strength is viable (bigger with strong hands), which they advise against online. [OPINION][LIVE examples inside an ONLINE/6-max episode] — [Upswing Level-Up podcast ep. 29, "Preflop Raise Sizes That Win"](https://upswingpoker.com/podcast/ep29-pfr-sizing/)
- Jonathan Little / PokerCoaching: versus a player who limp-calls any raise, "raise 10x or more" with AK, KK, AA. [OPINION][LIVE](snippet) — [PokerCoaching, "Cash Game Poker Strategy: 10 Tips"](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/cash-game-strategy-tips/)
- Why big sizes (solver view): in a 10%-rake, 2bb-cap, no-flop-no-drop solve at 125bb, AA earns 11.02bb opening 3x vs 10.34bb opening 2x, but "even AA wins less by raising 7x than it does by raising 2x, a whopping 2bb less" against equilibrium responders; at 300bb AA's best size is 4x. The author concludes larger raises "may be good exploits from late position against loose, passive opponents (i.e., most live cash games)". [SOLVER][cash, raked; not population data] — [Andrew Brokos, "How to Respond to Large Preflop Raises", GTO Wizard blog, Feb 2025](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-to-respond-to-large-preflop-raises-in-poker/)

**Open-limping and over-limping (sources disagree)**
- Never limp outside the blinds: Miller (above). Lee Jones: don't limp weak hands; limping T7s from middle position is a "-EV play". Little: limp-calling a raise is "one of the costliest habits" - raise first in or fold. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Lee Jones, "3 Deadly Techniques to Punish Limpers in Live Cash Games", PokerCoaching 2023](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/3-deadly-techniques-to-punish-limpers-in-live-cash-games/); [Jonathan Little, "When Limping in Poker Makes Sense", PokerCoaching 2024](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/limping-in-poker/)
- Over-limping is acceptable: Little allows it in late position with speculative hands (example 5s4s on the button) after "two or three players" limp, "in passive games with deep stacks". Upswing lists over-limp candidates as suited connectors (54s-98s), small suited aces (A2s-A4s) and pairs too weak to raise (22-99), more so with more limpers, closer to the button, and fewer aggressive players behind; avoid it outside late position/SB. SplitSuit's default with small/mid pairs in mid-late position is to limp behind and take the cheap flop unless a raise would get 0-1 callers. Roy Cooke: in loose-passive games with little raising, limping pairs is "generally correct". [OPINION][LIVE] — [Little](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/limping-in-poker/); [Upswing vs multiple limpers](https://upswingpoker.com/vs-multiple-limpers/); [Upswing passive tables](https://upswingpoker.com/passive-poker-tables-strategy/); [James Sweeney, "Raising Preflop In A Limp-Fest Pot", Red Chip Poker](https://redchippoker.com/raising-preflop-limp-fest-pots/); [Roy Cooke, Card Player 29/24](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66342-jason-koon-29-24/articles/22924-learning-no-limit-from-scratch-playing-sets-and-set-mining-correctly-the-basics)
- Data point on the dispute: with 2c2s on the button behind one limper, immediate ("momentary") EV of limping is -$0.38 vs +$0.63 for raising to $10; raising beat limping for almost every hand, the exceptions being 63s, 53s, 65o, 54o. With 3d3s on the button facing three limpers both options are slightly negative before implied value (raise -$0.19, limp -$0.28) and about +$0.31 / +$0.22 after an estimated skill edge. [DATA-derived model][LIVE] — [Selbrede, PokerNews 2017](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/preflop-raising-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-cash-games-29209.htm); [Selbrede, "Implied Value in Live $1/$2", PokerNews 2018](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/implied-value-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-games-29726.htm)

**3-betting and facing 3-bets**
- Low-stakes pool "never" 3-bets light: opponents re-raise only "the top 5% of their hands" (so their 3-bets are strong and can be folded to). [OPINION][cash $1-$3/$2-$5] — [Alex Fitzgerald, "10 Mistakes Low-Stakes Players Make", Card Player 38/11, 2025](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/sam-greenwood-shares-his-punt-of-the-day-strategy/articles/card-player-magazine-38-11-strategy-10-mistakes-low-stakes-players-fitzgerald)
- Live pool "tends to call 3-bets too wide", so 3-bet a merged/linear value range (suited broadways, strong Ax) rather than adding bluffs; and 3-bet more of your continuing range because pots that end preflop pay no rake. [OPINION + SOLVER charts][LIVE $1/$2-$2/$5] — [Jake Godshall, "2 Winning Ways to Adjust Your Preflop Strategy in Low Stakes Live Poker", Upswing 2022](https://upswingpoker.com/live-preflop-rake-adjustments/)
- Example linear ranges on splashy tables: CO vs MP open 3-bet 99+, ATs+, KJs+, QJs and flat suited connectors, suited aces, 22-88; from the blinds squeeze AJo, ATs+, KTs+, QTs+, JTs, 99+. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Upswing splashy tables](https://upswingpoker.com/splashy-live-games/)
- 3-bet sizing: "3x in position / 4x out of position" is called "not set in stone" - go bigger when the open is small. Examples: squeeze at $2/$5 about $70 (14bb) on the button, $75-$80 from the SB; 4-bets about 2.3-2.7x; avoid oversized 4-bets. [OPINION][ONLINE/6-max baseline with live quiz examples] — [Upswing podcast ep. 29](https://upswingpoker.com/podcast/ep29-pfr-sizing/)
- Versus large live opens, the solver wants "a higher threshold for 3-betting larger raises" and "a slightly smaller multiple of their raise"; raise size mostly changes calling frequency, not 3-bet frequency. [SOLVER][cash, raked] — [Brokos, GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-to-respond-to-large-preflop-raises-in-poker/)
- Live 3-bet outcome data: after a 3-bet everyone folds preflop only 26% of the time, and in 28% of cases a player behind cold-calls or 4-bets. [DATA][LIVE, higher-stakes sample] — [Hand2Note blog](https://hand2note4-com-test-dzf5dpdueud7cqdm.westeurope-01.azurewebsites.net/Blog/Databases/online-vs-live-poker-insights)
- Limp-reraises from live players are almost always the top of range ("you've usually run into KK+ and are happy to fold"). [OPINION][LIVE] — [Lee Jones, PokerCoaching](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/3-deadly-techniques-to-punish-limpers-in-live-cash-games/)
- Counter-view on folding to 3-bets: "most live players fold far too often to 3-bets, even when they'll have position". [OPINION](snippet) — [PokerListings, "Five Mistakes That Can Cause Players to Get Stuck on Small Stakes"](https://www.pokerlistings.com/blog/five-mistakes-that-can-cause-players-to-get-stuck-on-small-stakes)

**Blind defence and rake**
- Raked solves cut the bottom of the opening range and tighten big-blind defence further ("removes a ton of the worst hands"); exact percentages are in chart images only. Timed-rake games allow wider play. [SOLVER][LIVE framing, 200bb HJ charts] — [Godshall, Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/live-preflop-rake-adjustments/)
- In a 10%-rake/2bb-cap solve at 125bb: BTN vs a 4x open "folds virtually all hands with a card below a Ten, and even AJo"; vs 7x it folds TT and AJs; in raked games calling small raises is undesirable except from the BB; unraked BB calls vs 5x "vanish almost entirely" in favour of a linear 3-bet range. [SOLVER][cash] — [Brokos, GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-to-respond-to-large-preflop-raises-in-poker/)
- Live low-stakes rake "can be upwards of 25% of the pot on the flop"; general rule "loosen pre-flop raising ranges and tighten pre-flop calling ranges"; chopping blinds can help. [OPINION](snippet) — [Upswing, "What is Rake in Poker & How Does It Impact Strategy?"](https://upswingpoker.com/rake-poker-strategy-adjustments/)
- Forum counterpoint: rake mostly affects calling ranges and has "minimal" effect on opening/3-bet/4-bet widths. [OPINION][ONLINE](snippet) — [Run It Once thread, "Rake effect on Preflop ranges"](https://www.runitonce.com/nlhe/rake-effect-on-preflop-ranges/)
- Splashy tables: do not defend the BB wide against big raises; prefer hands that dominate or make the nuts (suited Ax, suited connectors, pairs). [OPINION][LIVE] — [Upswing splashy tables](https://upswingpoker.com/splashy-live-games/)

**Straddles**
- 85% of $2/$5 and $5/$10 games in the live database included a straddle; straddled games had about twice the variance of comparable non-straddled games. [DATA][LIVE, higher-stakes sample] — [Hand2Note blog](https://hand2note4-com-test-dzf5dpdueud7cqdm.westeurope-01.azurewebsites.net/Blog/Databases/online-vs-live-poker-insights)
- Straddle halves effective depth ($400 at $1/$2 = 200bb, but 100 "straddle blinds" with a $4 straddle); raise sizes scale to the straddle (about $12-$16 over a $4 straddle); open tighter; the "family limp" behind the straddle is described as the most common straddled-pot leak. [OPINION][LIVE](snippet; SEO-style sites, low confidence) — [DeucesCracked straddle guide](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-straddle-strategy-live-games-guide); [betterpoker "Strategy Against a Straddle"](https://betterpoker.vercel.app/learn/cash-game-specialization/straddles-and-loose-games/strategy-vs-straddle)

**Stack depth**
- Scale all preflop sizes down at ~50bb and slightly up at ~200bb; iso size ~3bb at 40bb effective and larger at 250bb. [OPINION][ONLINE/6-max] — [Upswing podcast ep. 29](https://upswingpoker.com/podcast/ep29-pfr-sizing/)
- At 300bb (raked solve) the BTN raises a bit more and the blinds 3-bet less; unraked 300bb BB keeps flatting even JJ/AKo/AQs some of the time vs 5x. [SOLVER][cash] — [Brokos, GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-to-respond-to-large-preflop-raises-in-poker/)
- Seat/depth rule: buy in shallow when out of position to the good regulars, deep when you have position on them. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Evan Jarvis, Card Player 33/8](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66433-antonio-esfandiari-33-8/articles/23962-how-to-win-more-money-playing-live-poker-four-game-changing-strategies)

### Inferences
- A defensible simulator baseline for open sizing at $1/$2-$1/$3 is 4-5bb first-in plus 1bb per limper (plus ~1bb more out of position), escalating until the average flop is heads-up or three-way. Sources range from "3-4x + 1" (Lee Jones, in position) to "5x + 1" (Sweeney), so any open between roughly 4bb and 7bb first-in should not be flagged as an error on size alone.
- The solver result that AA loses EV with very large opens assumes opponents fold correctly; Selbrede's data (limpers call a $10 raise 56% of the time; calling frequency assumed roughly size-insensitive) is the reason coaches still recommend large sizes. The simulator's verdict on sizing should therefore depend on the modelled callers' elasticity, not on equilibrium.
- Because the pool 3-bets ~top 5% and rarely bluffs, "folding AQ/JJ-type hands to a tight player's 3-bet" follows from the cited premises, but no accessed source states a concrete continue range versus a nit's 3-bet.
- Limping disagreement resolves roughly by hand class and position: nobody defends open-limping; over-limping small pairs / suited connectors / small suited aces in late position behind 2+ limpers is endorsed by Little, Upswing, Sweeney and Cooke and opposed by Miller (who values simplicity). The simulator should treat late-position over-limps with those classes as "acceptable variant", and open-limps and limp-calls as leaks.

### Gaps
- No accessible source gave concrete position-by-position opening ranges (hand lists or percentages) specifically for live 8-10 handed raked games. Miller's "The Course" charts, Upswing's live charts and Little's book charts are behind paywalls/images. The percentages above are generic full-ring figures from low-quality snippets.
- No quantified BB defence range versus 4-6x live opens with $5+ rake beyond the solver image charts.
- No source quantified how ranges should change at 40bb vs 100bb vs 200bb+ in live full-ring games; the only depth guidance is from online 6-max material and one solver article.
- Straddle strategy: only low-quality snippets; no data on how live pools actually play straddled pots beyond their prevalence.
- Responding to 3-bets "by player type" is asserted qualitatively only; no frequencies.

## 2. Isolation raising over limpers (sizing, ranges, and what to do when isolation fails)

### Takeaway
Sizing formulas cluster at 4bb + 1bb per limper in position and about 1bb more out of position, with Sweeney at 5bb + 1 and several coaches telling you to keep increasing until you actually thin the field. Ranges should be linear and get tighter as limpers are added (data: profitable button iso range falls from ~33% vs one limper to ~13% vs four); sources mostly say expect the pot to go multiway anyway and choose hands that play well at low SPR.

### Cited Findings
- Sizing formulas for live play: 4bb + 1/limper (Upswing; Selbrede); 4bb + 1/limper in position and add 1bb out of position, e.g. AJs on the button over two limpers at $1/$2 = $12 (Little); "3-4 times the big blind, plus one more for every limper" in late position, e.g. $25 at $2/$5 over two limpers with "$30 or even $35" acceptable, and 8x or more ($25-$35 at $2/$3) from the blinds over three limpers (Lee Jones); "5x+1x/limper", i.e. $18 over four limpers at $1/$2, with $20-$30 usually best in limp-fests and $10 inviting everyone / $40 getting no action (Sweeney); "4-5BB plus 1BB per additional limper" (Upswing live tips). [OPINION][LIVE] — [Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/vs-multiple-limpers/); [Selbrede](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/preflop-raising-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-cash-games-29209.htm); [Little](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/limping-in-poker/); [Lee Jones](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/3-deadly-techniques-to-punish-limpers-in-live-cash-games/); [Sweeney](https://redchippoker.com/raising-preflop-limp-fest-pots/); [Upswing, "8 Live Poker Tips"](https://upswingpoker.com/live-poker-tips-strategy/)
- Versus a habitual limper who will not fold, 6-8bb isolation; AKs in the cutoff after a limp-call, 14-16bb (warns against going larger). [OPINION][ONLINE baseline + live quiz] — [Upswing podcast ep. 29](https://upswingpoker.com/podcast/ep29-pfr-sizing/)
- Number of callers after a button raise to $10 over one EP/MP limper at Vegas $1/$2: everyone folds 23.1%, one caller 47.0%, two callers 25.7%, three callers 4.2%. From the BB over one limper, fold equity is 43.6%. [DATA-derived model][LIVE] — [Selbrede](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/preflop-raising-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-cash-games-29209.htm)
- Profitable (immediate-EV) isolation range: button vs one limper about 33% of hands (figure shows 33.6%); button vs four limpers 13.4%; BB vs one limper 27.6% (22+, A2+, KT+, QJ). A hand at about -$0.50 immediate EV may still be playable for implied value. [DATA-derived model][LIVE] — [Selbrede](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/preflop-raising-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-cash-games-29209.htm)
- "The more limpers there are in front of you, the tighter you should iso-raise"; position matters but less than limper count; 99-88 can be added versus weak limpers. [OPINION][LIVE+ONLINE] — [Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/vs-multiple-limpers/)
- Default raising range in a limp-fest: TT+ and AQ+ ("a pure default"), adding AJ, KQ, 99 when appropriate; raise far more from CO/BTN than from the blinds; avoid raising hands like KJs when the raise will bring five players to the flop. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Sweeney](https://redchippoker.com/raising-preflop-limp-fest-pots/)
- Single-limper example range from middle position versus a limp-folder with tight players behind: A9s, A5s, KTs, QTs, J9s, 98s, 87s, 76s, 66, 55 (plus everything stronger); tighten and size up versus multiple limpers; do not iso with any two cards. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Upswing passive tables](https://upswingpoker.com/passive-poker-tables-strategy/)
- Squeeze limpers with a linear range, "not including any bluffing hands"; examples 55 from the hijack, K9s from the cutoff ("good enough"), AJo from the BB. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Lee Jones](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/3-deadly-techniques-to-punish-limpers-in-live-cash-games/)
- Disagreement on width: Upswing's live tips say tighten in theory but "play looser against most limpers" in practice; Sweeney and "vs multiple limpers" say stay strong-weighted. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Upswing live tips](https://upswingpoker.com/live-poker-tips-strategy/); [Sweeney](https://redchippoker.com/raising-preflop-limp-fest-pots/)
- When isolation fails: "Expect a multiway pot even after you raise, rarely a heads-up one"; the first limper's call drags others in; multiway raised pots mean bigger pots, smaller SPR and "tougher continuation betting", hence the strong-weighted range. Out of position, "five-way flops are a mess", hence larger sizing. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Sweeney](https://redchippoker.com/raising-preflop-limp-fest-pots/); [Lee Jones](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/3-deadly-techniques-to-punish-limpers-in-live-cash-games/)
- After isolating, "don't pay off limpers who hit": when a limper raises or check-raises the turn, fold one pair. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Lee Jones](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/3-deadly-techniques-to-punish-limpers-in-live-cash-games/)
- Solver note: limping is not part of equilibrium strategies, so no exact solutions against limpers exist. [OPINION](snippet) — [PokerSkill glossary, "iso raise"](https://www.pokerskill.com/poker-glossary/iso-raise/)

### Inferences
- Selbrede's distribution implies that a standard 5bb iso over a single limper produces a heads-up pot only about half the time and a 3-4 way pot about 30% of the time; with multiple limpers the multiway share will be much higher. The simulator should therefore evaluate iso hands on their multiway playability, not just heads-up equity versus a limping range.
- A workable range rule consistent with all sources: one limper, in position: roughly top 25-33% (linear); two limpers: roughly top 15-20%; three or more limpers: roughly 10-13% (TT+/AQ+ core plus AJs/KQs/99), with small pairs, suited connectors and small suited aces shifting to over-limps in late position. The end points are from Selbrede and Sweeney; the middle value is interpolation, not sourced.
- Postflop after a failed isolation is covered only by general multiway advice (section 3); no source gives a dedicated "iso failed" plan.

### Gaps
- No source gives isolation ranges by position AND number of limpers as hand lists for live play (Upswing's chart is an image).
- No data on how live callers' frequency changes with iso size (Selbrede explicitly assumes it does not change much and has no data).
- No source quantifies c-bet frequency/sizing specifically in iso-raised pots that went 3-5 ways.

## 3. Multiway pots (3, 4, 5+ to the flop)

### Takeaway
Every source, solver and coach alike, says the same direction: tighter value thresholds, far fewer bluffs, smaller bets, more checking, nut potential over raw equity, and near-total respect for raises. Hard numbers exist only from solvers on 3-way pots (online 6-max, 8-max cEV, or MTT), not from live 4-5 way pots; top pair is described as "often worth only one street".

### Cited Findings

**How often it matters**
- In a live database, 55% of flops were multiway (24% three-way, 31% four-plus-way). [DATA][LIVE, higher-stakes/TAG sample] — [Hand2Note blog](https://hand2note4-com-test-dzf5dpdueud7cqdm.westeurope-01.azurewebsites.net/Blog/Databases/online-vs-live-poker-insights)
- Vegas $1/$2 flops contain "far more villains" than online flops, limped or raised, and the average final pot of raised hands that see a flop is 50% larger than online. [DATA][LIVE] — [Selbrede, PokerNews 2018](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/implied-value-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-games-29726.htm)

**Shared defence / bluffing math**
- Total fold frequency is the product of individual folds, so each defender's obligation shrinks: versus a 10%-pot bet a lone defender defends 91%, but eight defenders need only about 26% each; on Ac Tc 2s versus an overbet the average defence per player is 25% multiway vs 44% heads-up. [MATH + SOLVER][ONLINE/6-max, NL500 rake, mostly 200bb] — [Tombos21, "10 Tips for Multiway Pots in Poker", GTO Wizard blog 2023](https://blog.gtowizard.com/10-tips-multiway-pots-in-poker/)
- A half-pot bluff needs 33% combined folds; a typical range flops a made hand or draw about 63% of the time, so versus two or more opponents bluffing "becomes very hard to profit from", worse against over-callers; marginal value bets are also riskier because someone more often has two pair+. [MATH + OPINION][LIVE] — [Upswing splashy tables](https://upswingpoker.com/splashy-live-games/)
- "Almost never bluff without solid drawing equity, except on the river"; "stop range betting"; give up more with trash, tighten value thresholds, check back more medium hands. [SOLVER-derived heuristics][ONLINE/6-max] — [GTO Wizard 10 tips](https://blog.gtowizard.com/10-tips-multiway-pots-in-poker/)

**C-bet frequency and sizing (solver)**
- LJ open, SB and BB call (100bb, 8-max, chip-EV, 20-flop weighted subset): the raiser's checking frequency rises by 11 percentage points versus heads-up against the BB, and pot-sized c-bets fall from 18% of the time heads-up to 1.3% multiway; SB checks about 90% and leads 9.8% (mostly 33% pot) on boards that favour its range; the raiser's EV fell for every flop except A-K-9 two-tone where it keeps the nut advantage. [SOLVER][8-max cEV, no rake stated] — [JonnyLaw, "Playing In Position Against Two Callers", GTO Wizard blog 2026](https://blog.gtowizard.com/playing-in-position-against-two-callers/)
- Smaller sizes multiway: heads-up the button likes 125% pot on A-T-2 two-tone; with both blinds calling, the strongest 25% of their ranges beat even A2 (two pair) and AK, so overbets are reserved for clear nut advantage. [SOLVER][ONLINE/6-max] — [GTO Wizard 10 tips](https://blog.gtowizard.com/10-tips-multiway-pots-in-poker/)
- Secondary report: on an A-J-T flop the raiser mostly uses a 20% pot c-bet multiway even at 80bb; in one high-roller hand analysis the solver bet only about 11.7% of hands. [SOLVER][MTT/high-roller](snippet) — [PokerNews, "This is How The Pros Play Multiway Pots"](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/this-is-how-the-pro-s-play-multiway-pots-51467.htm); [PokerNews, Triton hand analysis](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/struggling-in-multiway-pots-gto-wizard-shows-the-answer-51069.htm)
- Counterpoint: against a very wide, call-heavy opponent a solver "betting more often can be right" because that range cannot realise equity later. [SOLVER](snippet) — [GTO Wizard, "Roundtable: Aha! Moments"](https://blog.gtowizard.com/roundtable_aha_moments_using_solvers/)

**Responding to bets and raises**
- Middle player (BTN) facing a quarter-pot c-bet on T-7-4 two-tone: heads-up about 10% fold / 73% call / 17% raise; with the BB still to act about 31% fold / 39% call / 30% raise - "tighter, more aggressive, and more linear". The player closing the action (BB) folds about 45% after a call but about 85% after a raise. On K-7-2 rainbow the middle player raises about 16% and calls 54%; BB folds about 68%. Flush draws and open-enders are never folded on T74tt. [SOLVER][MTT, ~40bb] — [Andrew Brokos, "Monkey in the Middle: 3-Way Pot Heuristics", GTO Wizard blog 2026](https://blog.gtowizard.com/monkey-in-the-middle-3-way-pot-heuristics/)
- "Play extremely tight against raises, since multiway raises are usually strong"; be selective calling with a caller in front or a player behind. [OPINION][LIVE+ONLINE] — [Upswing vs multiple limpers](https://upswingpoker.com/vs-multiple-limpers/)
- Treat multiple opponents as one "enemy unit"; play tight-passive, mostly check and fold; when betting be value-heavy with very little bluffing. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Upswing passive tables](https://upswingpoker.com/passive-poker-tables-strategy/)

**Hand-strength shifts**
- "Don't overvalue top pair in multiway pots. It is often worth only one street of value and may be a fold against a big bet plus a call or raise." [OPINION] — [Upswing vs multiple limpers](https://upswingpoker.com/vs-multiple-limpers/)
- With underpairs to the board in many-way pots, check and see what develops; if betting, bet small; call at least one turn bet only versus opponents who bluff with marginal hands. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Jonathan Little, comments on "Strategies for Beating Small Stakes Poker Cash Games"](https://jonathanlittlepoker.com/cashstrategies/)
- Miller's Skill #3: hand value depends on kicker, texture, number of opponents and tendencies; do not bet to "push opponents out" - a hand that wins before showdown gained nothing from being top pair; aim to reach showdown "charging your opponents the maximum value of your hand". [OPINION][LIVE] — [Miller, Card Player](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66294-cppt-foxwoods-28-2/articles/22227-three-skills-to-beat-1-2)
- Nut potential dominates: on As Qs 8h, 6s4s has poor nut potential and Ks7s strong; players without the nut advantage should be passive unless SPR is very low; multiway raises implied AND reverse implied odds and stronger hands are needed to stack off; favoured classes are suited connectors, suited gappers and nut-flush hands. [SOLVER-derived][ONLINE/6-max] — [GTO Wizard 10 tips](https://blog.gtowizard.com/10-tips-multiway-pots-in-poker/)

**Draws, protection, sets**
- Play draws more passively multiway (less fold equity); with two pair or better on drawy boards bet to build the pot and charge draws. [OPINION] — [Upswing vs multiple limpers](https://upswingpoker.com/vs-multiple-limpers/)
- Sets: on draw-heavy boards (J-T-8 two-tone) bet larger and raise more; on dry boards (T-6-2 rainbow) smaller bets draw more action; slowplay fails when opponents will call anyway. The goal is to make opponents commit pricing errors, not to shut them out. [OPINION] — [Roy Cooke, Card Player 29/24](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66342-jason-koon-29-24/articles/22924-learning-no-limit-from-scratch-playing-sets-and-set-mining-correctly-the-basics)
- Miller on flop commitment: avoid getting all-in on the flop with strong-but-vulnerable hands (example AhQh on Jh 8d 6h) "unless you hold a set"; call with strong draws and play turns and rivers where opponents make bigger errors. [OPINION][LIVE $2-$5] — [Miller, Red Chip Poker](https://redchippoker.com/how-live-regs-shoot-themselves-in-the-foot/amp/)

**Implied odds and equity realisation**
- You flop a set about 7.5:1 (12% "set or better"); set-mining value rises with more opponents and a cheaper entry, and "the propensity to acquire your opponent's stack is far more important ... than its size" (a player who will pay 12x your entry price with AA beats one who won't pay at 20x). Cooke calls the common stack-multiple rules "incomplete". [MATH + OPINION] — [Roy Cooke, Card Player](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66342-jason-koon-29-24/articles/22924-learning-no-limit-from-scratch-playing-sets-and-set-mining-correctly-the-basics); [Selbrede 2018](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/implied-value-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-games-29726.htm)
- Stack-multiple rules of thumb for set-mining range from 10x to 20x the call (15x common; 20x+ out of position; 10-15x if the opponent stacks off readily). [OPINION][mostly online-origin](snippet) — [PokerNews, "Set Mining 101"](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/set-mining-101-do-you-have-the-right-odds-to-hit-your-set-35667.htm); [BlackRain79, "Is Set Mining Profitable?"](https://www.blackrain79.com/2019/12/is-set-mining-profitable.html)
- Button in a limped Vegas pot: skill plus position is worth roughly $0.60-$1.00 per button played ("probably a best-case estimate"), enough that "every" high-implied-odds hand such as suited connectors is worth playing there; but multiway flops make it harder to realise both skill and positional edge, and JTo facing three limpers is -$1.23 to raise vs -$0.25 to limp. [DATA-derived estimate][LIVE] — [Selbrede 2018](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/implied-value-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-games-29726.htm)
- Position is worth more multiway: CO opens 2.5bb at 200bb, BTN defends 8% wider than SB; "being out of position twice is very bad"; BB should not defend much wider after SB calls. [SOLVER][ONLINE/6-max] — [GTO Wizard 10 tips](https://blog.gtowizard.com/10-tips-multiway-pots-in-poker/)

**Theory caveat**
- Multiway Nash strategies are not guaranteed unexploitable or to hold their EV; current 3-way postflop solving is commercially available, more players are not. [SOLVER theory] — [GTO Wizard 10 tips](https://blog.gtowizard.com/10-tips-multiway-pots-in-poker/); [PokerNews on GTO Wizard multiway preflop solver, 2026](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2026/02/gto-wizard-launches-multiway-preflop-solver-50550.htm)

### Inferences
- Per-opponent fold needed for a pure bluff to break even (my arithmetic from the cited formula, assuming independent, equal folding): one-third pot needs 25% combined = 50% each vs two, 63% each vs three, 71% each vs four; half pot needs 33% combined = 58% / 69% / 76% each; pot needs 50% combined = 71% / 79% / 84% each. Against callers who each continue ~60% of the time (the Upswing 63% figure), a pure flop bluff into three or more players succeeds roughly 5% of the time (0.37^3) - effectively never profitable. The simulator should treat no-equity c-bets into 3+ live opponents as an error by default.
- A reasonable multiway c-bet heuristic for the simulator, consistent with the solver direction but not a sourced number: 3-way, bet top pair good kicker+ and strong draws at ~33-50% pot and check most else; 4-way+, bet for value only with two pair+ / strong top pair on dry boards and nut-type draws, check the rest. Live coaches (Miller, section 4) would bet larger with value when callers are inelastic - the size disagreement is "solver small" vs "exploit large", and it is resolved by the callers' elasticity in the model.
- "Overpairs become bluff-catchers" is supported indirectly: Upswing's top-pair statement, Little's "people raise when they love their hands", Lee Jones' fold-one-pair-to-turn-raises, and GTO Wizard's higher stack-off thresholds. No source gave a numeric threshold (e.g. number of opponents or bet size) at which an overpair should fold.
- All solver numbers cited are for 3-way pots with competent ranges (blinds calling a raise). Live 4-5 way limped or iso-raised pots have wider, weaker ranges; directionally the same advice applies but magnitudes are untested.

### Gaps
- No solver or data work on 4-way or 5+-way postflop play was found; public tools stop at three players postflop.
- No live population data on multiway c-bet, fold-to-c-bet, or raise frequencies at $1/$2-$3/$5.
- No source quantifies equity realisation of specific hand classes (small pairs, suited connectors, suited aces) by number of players in live games; only qualitative statements plus Selbrede's button estimate.
- No quantified guidance on protection-vs-value bet sizing by player count.
- The widely quoted set-mining multiples are from snippets and online-era material; nothing live- and multiway-specific.

## 4. Value betting (sizing vs stations and nits, overbets, thin river value, inelastic ranges, pot control)

### Takeaway
The consensus exploit is: bet bigger and thinner for value because calling ranges are wide and relatively size-insensitive, and bet-fold the river because raises are almost never bluffs. Specific size recommendations are sparse and mostly qualitative; sources split on whether live players will actually call overbets.

### Cited Findings
- Thin river value threshold: a river value bet is profitable if you are good "just over 50 percent of the time" when called; river value bets are "almost like freerolls" because low/mid-stakes players raise-bluff the river so rarely that you can bet thin and fold to a raise. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Bart Hanson, Card Player 27/14, 2014](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66279-vanessa-selbst-27-14/articles/21967-crushing-live-poker-with-twitter)
- "Check-calling on the river is generally bad in live games" - err toward bet/folding unless inducing; check-callers "lose a ton of value"; legitimate check-calls are to induce with medium hands that worse won't call, or versus better players. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Bart Hanson, Card Player 25/11, 2012](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66201-amarillo-slim-25-11/articles/20633-tweet-crushing-live-poker-with-twitter)
- Players check back strong river hands because "they are not capable of folding if they get raised, so to counter this leak they do not bet." [OPINION][LIVE] — [Hanson 2014](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66279-vanessa-selbst-27-14/articles/21967-crushing-live-poker-with-twitter)
- Low-stakes players "never value bet enough" (check or bet too small on the river with strong top pair), and to the pool a value bet of "40% of the pot" looks small, i.e. it gets called. [OPINION][cash $1-$3/$2-$5] — [Fitzgerald, Card Player 2025](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/sam-greenwood-shares-his-punt-of-the-day-strategy/articles/card-player-magazine-38-11-strategy-10-mistakes-low-stakes-players-fitzgerald)
- "Live opponents call far more than players expect", especially with one pair and marginal bluff-catchers; checking back good river hands for fear of a raise is listed as a top live mistake. [OPINION][LIVE, tournament-oriented article] — [Calum Grant, "Six Live Poker Mistakes to Avoid in 2026", PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/6-live-poker-mistakes-to-avoid-40407.htm)
- Size is a lever, not a constant: with showdown-value hands "What if you bet $5? I bet you can get calls from worse", and "What if you bet 3x pot? I bet you can get folds from better"; regs check too many hands to showdown. [OPINION][LIVE $2-$5] — [Miller, Red Chip Poker](https://redchippoker.com/how-live-regs-shoot-themselves-in-the-foot/amp/)
- Inelastic ranges defined: a hand is inelastic if it calls or folds regardless of size; versus inelastic callers "if you bet small you are missing out on value". [OPINION][general/online origin](snippet) — [ThePokerBank, "Elastic and Inelastic Ranges"](https://www.thepokerbank.com/strategy/general/elastic-inelastic/)
- Caution: "recreational players are not idiots" - calling ranges do tighten on scary cards. [OPINION](snippet) — [PokerNews, "An Introduction to the Idea of Elasticity in Poker"](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/elasticity-poker-introduction-25967.htm)
- Overbets for value - disagreement: one Card Player panel says opponents "will usually not call an overbet, especially in live games, unless their hand strength is very high"; other pieces say river overbets exploit recreational players "who can't fold absolutely strong hands". [OPINION](snippets) — [Card Player, "Head Games: Cash Game Overbetting Dynamics"](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66253-mike-watson-26-21/articles/21475-head-games-cash-game-overbetting-dynamics); [PokerNews, "Advanced Overbetting Strategy When Playing Deep-Stacked"](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/advanced-overbetting-strategy-when-playing-deep-stacked-poke-34591.htm)
- Marc Goone (Hungry Horse): choose "a size where their opponent will raise off their strong stuff" and call with weak hands; opponents telegraph strength via sizing and "never check back very strong, nutted hands nearly enough"; a preferred value line is check back flop, bet turn, "bomb the river"; out of position as the raiser he now mostly checks, including strong hands, because opponents over-stab. In a test, six live pros winning 8-9bb/hour raised too many strong hands and called too many weak ones facing a c-bet. No percent-of-pot sizes given. [OPINION, citing 50,000+ hours of staked-player results at $2-$5 to $5-$10][LIVE] — [Card Player 37/23, "Marc Goone Explains How To Properly Size Your Bets", Oct 2024](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66553-tyler-ninja-blevins-37-23/articles/25224-marc-goone-explains-how-to-properly-size-your-bets)
- Sizing as a tell in the pool: slightly larger bet = wants a fold; slightly smaller = fears a raise; even smaller = seeking information; many $2-$5 players bet small to control the action. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Miller, "Four Skills to Beat $2-$5", Card Player 28/3](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66295-wynn-poker-room-28-3/articles/22241-four-skills-to-beat-2-5)
- Versus callers, barrel "marginal pairs" for value and check back pure junk (a pair of eights gains value versus a caller because the caller's weak hands are unpaired); versus folders do the reverse. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Miller, Card Player 28/3](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66295-wynn-poker-room-28-3/articles/22241-four-skills-to-beat-2-5)
- Pot control: multiway, "mix in more checks" with marginal value hands since two pair+ is more likely out; top pair multiway is often one street. [OPINION] — [Upswing splashy tables](https://upswingpoker.com/splashy-live-games/); [Upswing vs multiple limpers](https://upswingpoker.com/vs-multiple-limpers/)
- Turn sizing plan: size the turn so that about a half-pot bet is left for the river. [OPINION][LIVE](snippet; individual column not confirmed) — [Bart Hanson, "Crushing Live Poker With Twitter" series, Card Player](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66360-cppt-playground-poker-club-30-15/articles/23111-crushing-live-poker-with-twitter)

### Inferences
- For the simulator: against a "station" profile (inelastic, wide calls) the EV-maximising value size is large (two-thirds pot to pot and beyond) with a calling-range threshold of >50% equity when called; against a "nit" profile (elastic, tight) smaller sizes or a check are better. The 50%-when-called rule (Hanson) is the cleanest sourced test for flagging "missed thin value".
- Bet-fold should be the default river line with one-pair-type value hands versus the population; "check-call river with a hand that could have value-bet" is a flaggable leak category (missed value plus bluff-catching a range that rarely bluffs).
- The overbet-for-value disagreement suggests modelling elasticity by hand strength of the caller: stations holding strong-but-second-best hands (trips, straights, weak flushes) are inelastic to overbets; holding one pair they become elastic above roughly pot. This is inference; no source measures it.
- Goone's "check OOP as the raiser" is a minority, exploit-driven position versus the mainstream "bet your value hands"; it applies to heads-up pots against stab-happy opponents and is not a multiway recommendation.

### Gaps
- No measured live data on call frequency by bet size (elasticity curves) at these stakes; all claims are qualitative.
- No sourced percent-of-pot value sizings by street and opponent type from Miller, Hanson or Goone in the accessible material.
- Jonathan Little's player-type-specific sizing advice (from the book) was not accessible.

## 5. Bluffing (what remains profitable, what to drop, by opponent type)

### Takeaway
Cheap, early, heads-up bluffs that exploit fit-or-fold play (small flop c-bets, barrels after a check, stabs when the turn checks through) are endorsed; large late-street bluffs into callers and nearly all multiway bluffs are to be dropped. The dissent is Miller and Goone, who say regs over-fold enough that small-bet barrels and big river bluffs against specific fold-prone players still print.

### Cited Findings
- Barreling ("betting because your opponent checked") is a core skill: versus folders barrel most weak hands and check back hands with a little value; versus callers do not bluff but still bet, switching to marginal pairs. "Calling stations: don't bluff them, but still barrel them, changing only which hands you bet." [OPINION][LIVE $2-$5] — [Miller, Card Player 28/3](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66295-wynn-poker-room-28-3/articles/22241-four-skills-to-beat-2-5)
- Small bets work: bet about a third of the pot; "If you get a fold more than 25 percent of the time, you're gold"; $2-$5 regs fold too often to small flop and turn bets and give up too readily after calling the flop. Conversely, do not fold to small c-bets yourself. [MATH + OPINION][LIVE $2-$5] — [Miller, Red Chip Poker](https://redchippoker.com/how-live-regs-shoot-themselves-in-the-foot/amp/)
- River: when regs fold too much, turn showdown-value hands into bluffs; "Bluff double the pot" if a half-pot bluff fails. [OPINION][LIVE $2-$5] — [Miller, Red Chip Poker](https://redchippoker.com/how-live-regs-shoot-themselves-in-the-foot/amp/)
- Bluffs "print" when the flop checks through because recreational players almost never check back top pair, sets or two pair (example J-9-2 two-tone); on a dry turn where the nuts don't change "you can just bluff with impunity"; he will check-raise "anything with any shred of equity" versus small stabs. In position heads-up he almost always bets; out of position he almost always checks. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Marc Goone, Card Player 2024](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66553-tyler-ninja-blevins-37-23/articles/25224-marc-goone-explains-how-to-properly-size-your-bets)
- Pool tendencies that set bluff value: calling too much is "common" (two of the ten listed leaks); folding too much is "rare" (about "one player out of 10 or 20"); true maniacs are rare ("nine times out of 10" the label is wrong); the pool never overbet-bluffs (overbets are strong "nine times out of 10"). [OPINION][cash $1-$3/$2-$5] — [Fitzgerald, Card Player 2025](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/sam-greenwood-shares-his-punt-of-the-day-strategy/articles/card-player-magazine-38-11-strategy-10-mistakes-low-stakes-players-fitzgerald)
- It is a "misconception" that opponents will suddenly bluff big on the river after you have shown strength; focus on thin value, not bluff-catching big pots. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Hanson, Card Player 2012](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66201-amarillo-slim-25-11/articles/20633-tweet-crushing-live-poker-with-twitter)
- Multiway: pure bluffs are ineffective; bluff only with solid draw equity (river excepted); draws lose fold equity and should be played more passively. [SOLVER-derived + OPINION] — [GTO Wizard 10 tips](https://blog.gtowizard.com/10-tips-multiway-pots-in-poker/) [ONLINE/6-max]; [Upswing vs multiple limpers](https://upswingpoker.com/vs-multiple-limpers/)
- Exploit example from Upswing: versus a player who check-raises thin for value, bluff more on later streets and value bet thinner (Doug Polk quote); on passive live tables, stab when checked to. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Upswing live tips](https://upswingpoker.com/live-poker-tips-strategy/)
- Winning live TAGs in the database won 40% of pots when seeing the flop (WWSF 40%) with an aggression factor of 1.6. [DATA][LIVE, higher-stakes sample] — [Hand2Note blog](https://hand2note4-com-test-dzf5dpdueud7cqdm.westeurope-01.azurewebsites.net/Blog/Databases/online-vs-live-poker-insights)

### Inferences
- Profitable-bluff shortlist for the simulator (each traceable to a cited claim): (a) heads-up flop c-bet, small size, in position; (b) delayed c-bet / turn stab after the flop checks through (opponent's range is capped because strong hands always bet); (c) turn barrel versus fit-or-fold regs who called a small flop bet; (d) semi-bluffs with strong draws heads-up; (e) river bluff with a large size versus identified over-folders only.
- Drop list: (a) any no-equity bluff into 3+ players; (b) multi-barrel bluffs versus station profiles; (c) river bluff-raises (the pool reads raises as strong and stations still call); (d) bluff-catching large river bets/raises, which is the mirror image.
- The Miller/Goone "regs over-fold to small bets" claim and the Fitzgerald "the pool calls too much" claim are about different opponent segments (competent-ish regs vs recreational callers). The simulator's verdict on a bluff should be conditional on opponent type; a population-average opponent leans toward "calls too much".

### Gaps
- No frequencies: no source gives fold-to-c-bet, fold-to-turn-barrel or fold-to-river-bet numbers for live low stakes.
- "Specific scare cards" (e.g. ace/flush-completing turn probes) were not covered in any accessed source; presumably in the other researcher's solver-vs-pool deviation list.
- Hanson's and Miller's fuller treatments (podcasts, "The Course", "Playing the Player") were not accessible.

## 6. Stack depth and SPR planning

### Takeaway
SPR guidance exists mainly from Red Chip: top pair/overpairs are stack-offs at SPR about 2 or less (some say up to 3 heads-up), 3-6 is a judgement zone, and above ~6 one pair is not a stack-off hand; live raise sizes plus multiway callers produce low SPRs quickly. Miller dissents on flop stack-offs with vulnerable hands. Almost nothing accessible addresses 40bb short stacks or 300bb+ play at these stakes specifically.

### Cited Findings
- SPR = effective stack / pot on the flop (e.g. $150 / $50 = 3), computed once on the flop and planned for preflop. At 1-2 SPR "most of the time you should be sticking it in when you have anything even slightly decent"; "As a pure default two SPR and below, top pair I'm definitely going to be getting it in" (also overpairs and good draws); above about 6 "you just kinda don't worry about it too much". [OPINION][LIVE-aware] — [James Sweeney, Red Chip Poker SPR podcast](https://redchippoker.com/spr-stack-to-pot-ratio-podcast/)
- Live opens and 3-bets are larger so SPRs shrink: 3-bet/squeeze pots often land around 1-2.5 SPR live vs 3-4 online. Worked example: $480 effective, two limpers, $25 raise, $85 3-bet called, pot ~$200 with ~$400 behind = SPR 2; with top pair weak kicker on A-K-6 rainbow, hero bets $100 and is shoved on - Sweeney says call. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Red Chip SPR podcast](https://redchippoker.com/spr-stack-to-pot-ratio-podcast/)
- Alternative threshold: with top pair top kicker be happy all-in at SPR 3 or less versus a single opponent; 3-6 is a grey zone. [OPINION](snippet; exact Red Chip page not confirmed - likely glossary/cheat-sheet) — [Red Chip Poker glossary](https://redchippoker.com/poker-glossary-terms-definitions/)
- Live counter-adjustment: in loose live games "3 and 4 spr can easily be folded with one pair hands to aggression", because players almost never slow-play big hands in bloated pots; multiway you can have different SPRs against different opponents. [OPINION][LIVE](snippet; exact Red Chip page not confirmed - likely "Adjusting To Really Fishy Tables") — [Red Chip Poker, "Adjusting To Really Fishy Tables"](https://redchippoker.com/adjusting-to-fishy-tables/)
- Miller: $2-$5 regs get all-in on the flop too easily with strong-but-vulnerable hands and are often "on the wrong side of a flip"; avoid flop stack-offs unless you hold a set, and take draws to later streets. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Miller, Red Chip Poker](https://redchippoker.com/how-live-regs-shoot-themselves-in-the-foot/amp/)
- Multiway raised pots have smaller SPR, which is why limp-fest raising ranges should be strong-weighted (TT+, AQ+). [OPINION][LIVE] — [Sweeney, limp-fest article](https://redchippoker.com/raising-preflop-limp-fest-pots/)
- Multiway requires stronger hands to stack off (higher reverse implied odds); players without nut advantage stay passive "unless SPR is very low". [SOLVER-derived][ONLINE/6-max] — [GTO Wizard 10 tips](https://blog.gtowizard.com/10-tips-multiway-pots-in-poker/)
- Depth and win rate: in the live database, TAG win rates at $5-$10 and below rose with depth - 57 bb/100 at 0-300bb, 67 at 300-600bb, 98 at 600-1000bb (variance rising too). [DATA][LIVE, higher-stakes/TAG/unraked sample; not casino $1/$2 conditions] — [Hand2Note blog](https://hand2note4-com-test-dzf5dpdueud7cqdm.westeurope-01.azurewebsites.net/Blog/Databases/online-vs-live-poker-insights)
- Sizing by depth: shrink sizes at ~40-50bb, enlarge at 200-250bb. [OPINION][ONLINE/6-max] — [Upswing podcast ep. 29](https://upswingpoker.com/podcast/ep29-pfr-sizing/)
- Little attributes most of the achievable edge at $1/$2 to "avoiding poor stack-offs and stealing pots when no one is interested". [OPINION][LIVE] — [Jonathan Little, comments](https://jonathanlittlepoker.com/cashstrategies/)

### Inferences
- Typical SPRs the simulator will generate (my arithmetic, rake ignored): $1/$2, $200 stacks, open to $10 - heads-up with one caller pot ~$23, SPR ~8; three-way (two callers, blinds fold) pot $33, SPR ~5.8; five-way (three callers plus BB) pot $51, SPR ~3.7. So ordinary multiway raised pots sit in the 3-6 "grey zone", where the live-specific sources say one pair should fold to real aggression (raises, big turn/river bets) rather than stack off.
- Commitment rule consistent with sources: SPR <=2 heads-up, top pair+/overpair commits; SPR 2-4, commit versus one opponent betting/bluff-prone, but fold one pair to a raise from a passive player or in a multiway pot; SPR >6, one pair plays for two streets of value and pot control.
- Short stacks (40bb): opening 5bb creates SPR ~3 heads-up and ~2 three-way, so top pair is effectively committed versus them and suited connectors/small pairs lose implied odds; this follows from the SPR sources but is not stated by them.

### Gaps
- No accessed source addresses playing against 40bb short-stackers or 300bb+ deep stacks in live $1/$2-$3/$5 specifically (Miller's and Hanson's deep-stack material not reached).
- No data on how often live low-stakes players stack off with one pair by SPR.
- "Professional No-Limit Hold'em" (origin of SPR target ranges) was not consulted; the thresholds above come from Red Chip podcast/snippets only.

## 7. Table and seat selection, and share of edge attributed to it

### Takeaway
Advice is uniform (soft table, loose players on your right, tight players on your left) but no source quantifies the live effect; the only numbers are online figures or unsourced estimates. Benchmarks for a live winner cluster at 5-10 bb/hour, with data from a selected high-stakes sample showing ~15 bb/hour unraked.

### Cited Findings
- 90% of $2-$5 and $5-$10 tables in the live database had "at least two obvious fish"; 57% had three to five. [DATA][LIVE, sample includes streamed-style/high-stakes games; definition of "fish" not given] — [Hand2Note blog](https://hand2note4-com-test-dzf5dpdueud7cqdm.westeurope-01.azurewebsites.net/Blog/Databases/online-vs-live-poker-insights)
- Table selection: ask who has the money and how they got it; "You're better off playing at a table with less money but more opportunities to win." Seat selection: loose players to your right, tight players to your left; sitting directly left of a weak player gives first chance to isolate. No numbers. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Evan Jarvis, Card Player 33/8, 2020](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66433-antonio-esfandiari-33-8/articles/23962-how-to-win-more-money-playing-live-poker-four-game-changing-strategies)
- Soft-table signs: alcohol, groups playing for fun, loud players, many multiway pots; softest late at night and weekends. Playing faster raises hourly. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Upswing live tips](https://upswingpoker.com/live-poker-tips-strategy/)
- Prefer timed-rake games to pot-raked games "when possible" - "typically a much better deal for players". [OPINION][LIVE] — [Godshall, Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/live-preflop-rake-adjustments/)
- Position value (related to seat): online, the button is worth about 13 BB/100 more than UTG for experienced players; in Vegas $1/$2 the button's positional value is estimated at about $0.26 per dealt button, reduced by multiway flops. [DATA (online, ~6M hands NL100) + estimate (live)] — [Selbrede 2018](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/implied-value-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-games-29726.htm)
- Win-rate benchmarks: Little ~10 bb/hour at $5/$10-$10/$20 and believes >10 bb/hour "easily possible" at $1/$2 (own $1/$2 sample ~30 hours); Goone's test pros win 8-9 bb/hour at $2-$5+; Goone set out to show $100/hour (20 bb/hour) in a $5-$5 $1,000-cap game against a common belief that $50/hour is the ceiling (no result reported); TAGs in the Hand2Note live sample average 51 bb/100 (~15 bb/hour), with $2-$5 at 95 bb/100 ($118/hour), rake excluded; live pace 20-25 hands/hour. [DATA for Hand2Note (selected sample); OPINION/self-report otherwise] — [Little comments](https://jonathanlittlepoker.com/cashstrategies/); [Goone, Card Player](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66553-tyler-ninja-blevins-37-23/articles/25224-marc-goone-explains-how-to-properly-size-your-bets); [Hand2Note blog](https://hand2note4-com-test-dzf5dpdueud7cqdm.westeurope-01.azurewebsites.net/Blog/Databases/online-vs-live-poker-insights)
- "A solid, sustainable live cash win rate is roughly 5 to 10 big blinds an hour"; 25-35 hands/hour. [OPINION](snippet; tracking-app guide) — [Poker Analytics, live win-rate guide](https://www.poker-analytics.net/guides/live-poker-win-rate.html)
- Online-only magnitudes for table selection: a strong player might win 2-3 bb/100 among regulars vs 10-15 bb/100 with two recreational players; a claimed Hand2Note figure of regulars' edge rising from ~0.3 to 5.5 bb/100 when one recreational sits. [OPINION / unverified DATA][ONLINE](snippets; SEO-style sites, low confidence) — [DeucesCracked table selection](https://www.deucescracked.com/poker/strategy/table-selection); [VIP-Grinders $1/$2 guide](https://www.vip-grinders.com/the-ultimate-1-2-no-limit-holdem-cash-games-poker-strategy-guide/)

### Inferences
- The Hand2Note $2-$5 figure (95 bb/100) is not transferable to casino $1/$2-$3/$5: stacks of 500-1000bb, 85% straddled, no rake deducted, players selected for TAG stats, and likely private/streamed games. Use 5-10 bb/hour as the sourced "solid winner" range for calibration of the simulator's expected hourly.
- With rake around $5-$7 per raised pot and ~25-30 hands/hour, rake paid by a tight-aggressive player is plausibly several bb/hour, comparable in size to the win rate itself - which is why sources stress rake-aware calling ranges and timed-rake games. This is inference; no source computed it.
- Since no source quantifies it, the simulator should not attribute a numeric share of win rate to table/seat selection; at most it can show opponent-mix sensitivity from its own model.

### Gaps
- No live-specific measurement of how much win rate comes from table or seat selection; Berkey/Solve for Why and Hanson commentary on this was not reached.
- No reliable large-sample win-rate distribution for raked $1/$2-$3/$5 casino games (tracker-app aggregate data were not found).

## 8. Most common and most expensive leaks of developing players

### Takeaway
No source ranks leaks by bb/100 or $/hour. Sources converge most strongly on: (1) paying off big turn/river bets and raises, (2) playing too many hands preflop (limping, limp-calling, loose calls out of position), and (3) missing value (checking back, under-sizing); Miller explicitly orders his skills as preflop discipline first, not paying off second, hand valuation third.

### Cited Findings
- Miller's ordered skill list for $1-$2 ("the first skill is the most important"): #1 simple tight preflop strategy; #2 don't pay people off - "When people make big bets and raises on the turn and river, they aren't bluffing" ... "If you should fold, you should fold. Every time."; #3 assess hand value correctly. For $2-$5 he adds #4 barreling, #5 board texture, #6 live reads (bet sizing), #7 emotional numbing (tilt/loss tolerance). [OPINION, explicitly ordered][LIVE] — [Miller, Card Player 28/2](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66294-cppt-foxwoods-28-2/articles/22227-three-skills-to-beat-1-2); [Miller, Card Player 28/3](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66295-wynn-poker-room-28-3/articles/22241-four-skills-to-beat-2-5)
- Miller's $2-$5 reg leaks: root cause too many hands preflop; then (1) getting all-in on the flop too easily, (2) folding too often to small flop/turn bets, (3) checking too many hands to showdown instead of choosing a bet size. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Miller, Red Chip Poker](https://redchippoker.com/how-live-regs-shoot-themselves-in-the-foot/amp/)
- Fitzgerald's ten low-stakes mistakes with prevalence labels: calling too much (common); calling slightly too much (common); folding too much (rare - one in 10-20 players); bluffing too much (rare); never 3-betting beyond the top 5% (common); never overbet-bluffing (common); hero-calling all rivers - calling big river bets with mediocre two pair, sets or straights (common); never value betting enough (common); tilting (common); playing too many hours unfocused (common). [OPINION, prevalence-labelled not cost-ranked][cash $1-$3/$2-$5] — [Fitzgerald, Card Player 38/11](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/sam-greenwood-shares-his-punt-of-the-day-strategy/articles/card-player-magazine-38-11-strategy-10-mistakes-low-stakes-players-fitzgerald)
- Hanson: check-calling rivers instead of bet-folding; not value betting thin; fearing a river raise that is almost never a bluff. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Hanson 2012](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66201-amarillo-slim-25-11/articles/20633-tweet-crushing-live-poker-with-twitter); [Hanson 2014](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66279-vanessa-selbst-27-14/articles/21967-crushing-live-poker-with-twitter)
- Little: "most of the time, people raise when they love their hands" - fold hands that cannot beat a hand the opponent would love; limp-calling is one of the costliest habits; limp-reraising premiums is fine only versus weak live players. Against larger bets "folding is almost certainly the best play" ("Stop Being So Sticky", snippet). [OPINION][LIVE] — [Little comments](https://jonathanlittlepoker.com/cashstrategies/); [Little, limping article](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/limping-in-poker/); [Little, "Stop Being So Sticky", Card Player](https://www.cardplayer.com/poker-news/poker-strategy-jonathan-little-stop-being-sticky)
- Lee Jones' three: limping weak hands; failing to raise/squeeze limpers; paying off limpers who hit (fold one pair to turn raises/check-raises). [OPINION][LIVE] — [Lee Jones, PokerCoaching](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/3-deadly-techniques-to-punish-limpers-in-live-cash-games/)
- Multiway-specific: overvaluing top pair; bluffing/range-betting into several players; calling raises too loosely; applying heads-up solver sizing multiway. [OPINION + SOLVER-derived] — [Upswing vs multiple limpers](https://upswingpoker.com/vs-multiple-limpers/); [GTO Wizard 10 tips](https://blog.gtowizard.com/10-tips-multiway-pots-in-poker/)
- Goone on "applying theory blindly": board-texture/range-advantage reasoning is "irrelevant in live poker"; "It's better to underestimate people than overestimate"; "People aren't exploiting you"; he has needed the theory lens against only two players in six years; even winning pros (8-9 bb/hour) raise too many strong hands and call too many weak ones versus c-bets. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Goone, Card Player](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66553-tyler-ninja-blevins-37-23/articles/25224-marc-goone-explains-how-to-properly-size-your-bets)
- Rake-blind calling (defending blinds and flatting too wide in raked games) saves "small amounts each session" when fixed. [SOLVER + OPINION][LIVE] — [Godshall, Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/live-preflop-rake-adjustments/)
- Checking back good river hands out of fear of a raise. [OPINION][LIVE] — [PokerNews, Six Live Poker Mistakes](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/6-live-poker-mistakes-to-avoid-40407.htm)
- Tilt / playing tired: drop from A-game to B/C-game; quit when focus goes. [OPINION][LIVE] — [Jarvis, Card Player](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66433-antonio-esfandiari-33-8/articles/23962-how-to-win-more-money-playing-live-poker-four-game-changing-strategies)
- Only quantified cost-like figures found: over-limping 22 on the button vs raising = about $1.01 per instance in immediate EV (+$0.63 vs -$0.38) at Vegas $1/$2; raising JTo into three limpers costs about $0.98 more than limping (-$1.23 vs -$0.25). [DATA-derived model][LIVE] — [Selbrede 2017](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/preflop-raising-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-cash-games-29209.htm); [Selbrede 2018](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/implied-value-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-games-29726.htm)

### Inferences
- Proposed ordering for the simulator's feedback categories. This ranking is MY synthesis from how many independent sources name the leak and from pot size at the decision point (river/turn pots are the largest); it is not a sourced cost ranking:
  1. Paying off big turn/river bets and raises with one pair / bluff-catchers (Miller #2, Fitzgerald #1/#2/#7, Hanson, Little, Lee Jones #3) - largest pots, near-zero bluff frequency.
  2. Too many hands preflop: open-limping, limp-calling, loose cold-calls and blind defence versus large raises in raked pots, especially out of position (Miller #1 and "root cause", Lee Jones #1, Little, Upswing/GTO Wizard rake work).
  3. Missed and under-sized value: checking back rivers, betting too small versus inelastic callers, check-calling instead of bet-folding (Hanson, Fitzgerald #8, PokerNews, Miller leak 3).
  4. Multiway errors: overvaluing top pair/overpairs, c-betting air into 3+ players, calling multiway raises (Upswing, GTO Wizard).
  5. Bluffing stations / multi-barrel bluffs into callers; "fancy play" (Miller barreling rules, Fitzgerald).
  6. Flop over-commitment with vulnerable one-pair hands and big draws at medium SPR (Miller leak 1; SPR sources).
  7. Not isolating limpers / under-sizing preflop so every pot goes multiway (Lee Jones #2, Upswing).
  8. Calling 3-bets too wide and not folding to tight players' 3-bets/limp-reraises (Fitzgerald #5, Godshall, Lee Jones).
  9. Over-folding to small bets and failing to stab at checked-through pots (Miller leak 2, Goone) - a leak of tightened-up regs rather than beginners.
  10. Slow-playing strong hands versus callers (implied by Goone: opponents "never check back very strong hands"; Cooke: slowplay fails when they will call anyway).
  11. Applying solver/GTO concepts blindly - range-betting, small solver sizings for value versus stations, balanced bluff-catching (Goone; GTO Wizard's own multiway caveats).
  12. Tilt, fatigue, unfocused volume (Miller #7, Fitzgerald #9/#10, Jarvis).
- The convergence on #1-#3 across authors with different commercial interests (Miller, Hanson, Little, Fitzgerald) is the strongest signal in this research despite all being opinion.
- "Slow-playing" and "fancy play syndrome" were named in the brief but no accessed source treats them as headline leaks with that wording; their placement above is inferential.

- Forum consensus (TwoPlusTwo LLSNL stickies, Reddit) could not be retrieved; Hungry Horse's and Crush Live Poker's own "top leaks" lists (video/podcast) were not accessible in text form.
