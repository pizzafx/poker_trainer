# Live low-stakes pools play loose, passive, largely unmeasured

The best available evidence says a live $1/$2-to-$3/$5 full-ring pool is very loose and very passive before the flop, and that almost nothing after the flop has ever been measured. The single tracked sample of the target pool, Steve Selbrede's hand-recorded Las Vegas $1/$2 notes ("a few hundred" hands, four rooms, published 2017), gives **VPIP 35-37%, PFR 6%, 28% call-a-raise, no positional adjustment, and 4.0 players per flop with 27% of flops five-handed or more** ([Selbrede, PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/donk-betting-in-small-stakes-live-no-limit-hold-em-28638.htm)). No accessible source publishes a tracked live low-stakes figure for c-bet, fold-to-c-bet, check-raise, bluff share by street, went-to-showdown, or call frequency by bet size; coaches agree on direction (fit-or-fold, raises on turn and river almost never bluffs, rivers under-bluffed, small value bets paid off) but give almost no percentages. The only other live dataset, Hand2Note's transcription of streamed games, covers $2/$5 to $100/$200, is deep and 85% straddled, and reports winners' statistics rather than the pool's ([Hand2Note](https://hand2note.com/Blog/Databases/online-vs-live-poker-insights)). As a result only one archetype, the loose-passive limper (about 35/6, calls an isolation raise about 56% of the time), rests on tracked data; every other archetype band is coach opinion or an online HUD convention, and the bands disagree by 5-10 points. The exploitative consensus is tight raise-or-fold preflop with 4-6bb opens, large and thin value betting, near-total respect for late-street raises, and very few bluffs outside cheap heads-up spots against capped ranges; every dispute among sources (overbets, 3-bet shape, multiway c-bet size, betting versus checking strong hands) reduces to a second opponent parameter that a single "pool" profile cannot represent. For the exact $2/$3 and $3/$5 blinds, the evidence points to Southern California structures: a fixed drop of about $6 plus $1 per hand and buy-in caps of 20-100bb, which is a different game from a 10%-capped, 100-300bb Las Vegas $1/$3 or $2/$5. The practical consequence for the simulator is that few numbers can be hard-coded as empirical; most opponent parameters must be exposed as labelled, tunable assumptions.

**Evidence tags used on every claim.** `[DATA-live]` = tracked hands from the target pool (in practice only Selbrede's Vegas $1/$2 and $2/$5 samples). `[DATA-live, other pool]` = tracked live hands from a different population (Hand2Note streamed games, winners only; or single-observer notes). `[DATA-live, author-reported]` = the author says it rests on live tracking but publishes no numbers or method. `[DATA-online]` = online databases; not shown to transfer. `[MODEL]` = an author's calculation built on tracked stats. `[SOLVER]` = equilibrium output. `[SOLVER-SIM]` = solver run against an assumed opponent; proves the exploit only if the opponent behaves as assumed. `[OPINION]` = coach or author experience, no sample. `[ANECDOTE]` = single hand or single player report. `[CALC]` = arithmetic done by the research team on sourced inputs, not itself sourced. "(snippet)" = seen only in a search-result excerpt; the page was not opened, so wording and attribution are unverified.

**Method limits that apply throughout.** Pages were read through a text-extraction tool; Selbrede's full stat tables are images and only numbers quoted in article text are reported. Not accessed: Ed Miller's books, Jonathan Little's cash-game book, Doug Hull, Tommy Angelo, Zach Elwood's books, Crush Live Poker member content, Hungry Horse Poker videos, Selbrede's "Donkey Poker" volumes (Vol. 2 reportedly holds the full Vegas $1/$2 and $2/$5 tables), TwoPlusTwo LLSNL threads (HTTP 403), Reddit, and PokerAtlas listings. Most named-coach material is 2012-2019; nothing found addresses whether today's pool matches.

Three derived values follow from the table and are arithmetic, not sourced `[CALC]`. VPIP 35-37% with PFR 6% means about five of every six voluntarily played hands enter by limping or calling. If each of nine players raises 6% of hands, nobody raises about 57% of the time (0.94^9), so somewhat over half of pots are unraised; no source states a limped-pot share directly. A 0.8% 3-bet frequency, if real, is narrower than QQ+/AK (2.6% of combos) and close to KK+ (0.9%). One extraction of Selbrede's preflop article also reported "four-way pot about 92%" and "8%" figures that looked inconsistent with the 43.6% fold rate; the consistent reading is that a button over-limp behind one limper goes four-way about 92% of the time because the blinds raise only 4.8% and 3.4% (0.952 x 0.966 = 0.92), and the 8% is the complement. That reconciliation has not been checked against the original figure.

Observed (as opposed to recommended) open sizes are barely documented. Coaches recommend 4bb plus 1bb per limper at $1/$2, i.e. $10/$12/$14/$16 `[OPINION]` ([Selbrede 2017d](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/preflop-raising-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-cash-games-29209.htm)); descriptions of what the pool does are "three to five times the big blind" `[OPINION]` ([Little, PokerCoaching 2026](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/5-tips-to-beat-small-stakes-cash-games-in-2026/)) and "$10-$20 at $1/$3" with three or four callers `[OPINION, forum]` ([PokerChipForum 2025](https://pokerchipforum.com/threads/scared-to-take-the-leap-starting-1-3.129852)). With a $5 straddle at $1/$2, the same players open to $15-$20 `[OPINION]` (snippet) ([PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/how-straddling-changes-the-game-four-strategic-consideration-19521.htm)).

Re-raising ranges are described unanimously as value-only, with no measured composition. Live 3-bet and 4-bet ranges are "weighted heavily towards value hands"; a typical tight live 4-bet range is AA, KK, AKs and a very tight one AA/KK `[OPINION]` ([Mathias, Upswing 2019](https://upswingpoker.com/crush-live-poker-games-exploitative-tactics/)). Opponents at $1/$3 and $2/$5 re-raise only "the top 5% of their hands" `[OPINION]` ([Fitzgerald, Card Player 2025](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/sam-greenwood-shares-his-punt-of-the-day-strategy/articles/card-player-magazine-38-11-strategy-10-mistakes-low-stakes-players-fitzgerald)). A limp-reraise "usually" shows KK+ `[OPINION]` ([Lee Jones, PokerCoaching](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/3-deadly-techniques-to-punish-limpers-in-live-cash-games/)), or AA, KK, QQ, AK from a stereotypical $1/$3 player `[OPINION]` ([Little, Card Player 2018](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66379-card-player-poker-tour-bestbet-jacksonville-31-8/articles/23352-combatting-a-three-bet-from-a-limper)). No source says how often a live 4-bet is a bluff.

**Sources disagree on how the pool responds to 3-bets, and the disagreement is unresolved.** Mathias says live players over-fold because they are unused to wide 3-bet ranges `[OPINION]` ([Upswing 2019](https://upswingpoker.com/crush-live-poker-games-exploitative-tactics/)). Fitzgerald says they fold to 3-bets "a wildly small percentage of the time", attributing it to saving face `[DATA-live, author-reported; backing-group and student hands, likely including tournaments]` ([Fitzgerald](https://jonathanlittlepoker.com/exploitativealex/)); Godshall says the pool "tends to call 3-bets too wide" `[OPINION]` ([Upswing 2022](https://upswingpoker.com/live-preflop-rake-adjustments/)). The only number, the 26% all-fold rate above, comes from streamed, deep, straddled games against winning regulars. Neither side is measured on anonymous $1/$2-$3/$5 tables.

### Postflop frequencies exist only as direction, never as numbers

No tracked live low-stakes figure was found for c-bet frequency, fold-to-c-bet by street, barrel frequency, check-raise frequency, donk-bet frequency, share of raises that are bluffs, or bet-when-checked-to frequency. What follows is the direction each source asserts.

On c-betting, Selbrede infers from 4.0 players per flop and a pool "less willing to bluff" that raisers c-bet bluff less, especially multiway, and offers only online NL50/NL100 reference values (about 75% heads-up, 65% multiway in position, from roughly 6M online hands) as an upper bound `[OPINION built on DATA-live; DATA-online for the reference]` ([Selbrede 2017c](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/donk-betting-in-small-stakes-live-no-limit-hold-em-28638.htm)). Mathias describes c-bets as "fit or fold", often large on the flop (example $35 into $41) and then stalling; inexperienced players "bet all of their Qx hands at 100% frequency" on a Q-high flop, leaving weak flop-checking ranges, yet often check strong hands such as AQ on later streets after betting the flop `[OPINION]` ([Upswing 2019](https://upswingpoker.com/crush-live-poker-games-exploitative-tactics/)). Marc Goone says recreational players almost never check back top pair, sets or two pair, and that they over-stab when checked to and reveal strength by size `[DATA-live, author-reported; 50,000+ hours of staked play at $2/$5-$5/$10, unpublished]` ([Goone, Card Player 2024](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66553-tyler-ninja-blevins-37-23/articles/25224-marc-goone-explains-how-to-properly-size-your-bets)).

**On folding, sources split, and the split is probably about hand class rather than a contradiction.** One camp says the pool over-folds: players who miss "will usually fold" to a c-bet and callers "will often fold to the turn" `[OPINION]` (snippet) ([Red Chip Poker](https://redchippoker.com/playbook)); live players over-fold later streets to barrels `[OPINION]` ([Upswing 2019](https://upswingpoker.com/crush-live-poker-games-exploitative-tactics/)); $2/$5 regulars fold too often to small flop and turn bets `[OPINION]` ([Miller, Red Chip](https://redchippoker.com/how-live-regs-shoot-themselves-in-the-foot/amp/)). The other camp says it will not fold: mid- and low-stakes players "have problems folding strong hands" `[OPINION]` ([Hanson, Card Player 2014](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1037161)); $1/$2 and $1/$3 players are "absurdly unwilling to fold" `[OPINION]` (snippet) ([Elwood](https://zacharyelwood.substack.com/p/some-thoughts-on-quick-reads-of-opponent)); calling too much is "common" and folding too much "rare", about one player in 10 to 20 `[OPINION]` ([Fitzgerald, Card Player 2025](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/sam-greenwood-shares-his-punt-of-the-day-strategy/articles/card-player-magazine-38-11-strategy-10-mistakes-low-stakes-players-fitzgerald)); and they do not consistently fold pairs after the turn `[DATA-live, author-reported]` ([Fitzgerald](https://jonathanlittlepoker.com/exploitativealex/)). The reading that reconciles them (air and weak draws fold to flop and turn bets; top pair or better does not fold; recreational players over-call while regulars over-fold) is the research team's inference; no source quantifies it. The only fold-to-c-bet numbers are online and should not be transplanted: 45% flop / 34% turn / 37% river from one forum user's NL10 database `[DATA-online]` (snippet) ([TwoPlusTwo](https://forumserver.twoplustwo.com/15/poker-theory-amp-gto/fold-c-bet-betting-frequency-1777835/)). With 4.0 players per flop, per-player online fold rates say nothing about how often a live c-bet takes the pot.

**The most repeated claim in the whole literature is that turn and river raises are almost never bluffs.** Hanson: late-street raises "are almost never bluffs at the mid to low stakes", and in about ten years he could not recall one river bluff-raise against an overbet `[OPINION]` ([Crush Live Poker 2016](https://crushlivepoker.com/articles/raises-to-overbets)); river check-raise bluffs are "almost nonexistent" `[OPINION]` ([Crush Live Poker 2012](https://crushlivepoker.com/articles/small-flop-rasies)). Mathias: check-raising "occurs relatively infrequently" and calling river raises loses money against most live players even at good pot odds `[OPINION]` ([Upswing 2019](https://upswingpoker.com/crush-live-poker-games-exploitative-tactics/)). Lee Jones: limpers "rarely bluff" with a raise or check-raise `[OPINION]` ([PokerCoaching](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/3-deadly-techniques-to-punish-limpers-in-live-cash-games/)). Flop raises are the exception: "usually when someone raises the turn or the river they have better than a one pair hand but not always on the flop" `[OPINION]` ([Hanson, Crush Live Poker 2012](https://crushlivepoker.com/articles/delayed-semi-bluffing)). None of these gives a frequency; "near zero on the river, low on the turn, non-trivial on the flop" is the most that is supported.

River betting is described as heavily under-bluffed, again without live numbers. Live players "under-bluff by a lot" on the river `[OPINION]` ([Upswing 2019](https://upswingpoker.com/crush-live-poker-games-exploitative-tactics/)); a pot-sized river bet needs 33% bluffs to be balanced and "most $1-$2 players simply won't be bluffing 33 percent of the time" `[OPINION with theory benchmark]` ([Miller, Card Player 2018](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66390-george-antonakos-wins-cppt-bike-main-event-31-18/articles/23459-bluff-catching-at-1-2)); the majority do not double-barrel bluff on normal boards `[DATA-live, author-reported]`, and "about 20-40% of players are capable of double barrel bluffing" `[OPINION; the author calls it "limited research"; it is a share of players, not a per-hand frequency]` ([Fitzgerald](https://jonathanlittlepoker.com/exploitativealex/)). It is a "common misconception" that opponents bluff big when checked to after hero has shown strength; facing a scare card an average player checks back one pair, and if he bets he "most likely" beats one pair `[OPINION]` ([Hanson, Crush Live Poker](https://crushlivepoker.com/articles/rarely-check-call-the-river)). A five-player review of winning live players against solver output found they over-raise strong hands on the flop, under-bluff turn and river, and over-fold to big river bets `[OPINION from a five-player review, no percentages]` ([BluffAces summary of Hungry Horse](https://bluffaces.com/videos/there-are-no-good-players-in-live-poker/)). The one table of measured river bluff shares by bet size (heads-up small pots: 12/16/19/22/25% bluffs across rising sizes against equilibrium shares of about 18/25/30/33/37%; multiway: 7% on block bets against 18%, 9% at about half pot against 25%) comes from an article built on 500NL-10000NL online regulars, and the provenance of that particular table is not stated `[DATA-online, provenance unclear]` ([Dan B., Upswing 2026](https://upswingpoker.com/3-cash-game-exploits-regs-backed-by-data/)). Use it for direction only.

Thin value gets paid. At $1/$2 "a small value bet ... is likely to get called" and most players check-call rather than bet-fold the river `[OPINION]` ([Hanson, Crush Live Poker](https://crushlivepoker.com/articles/rarely-check-call-the-river)); to this pool a value bet of "40% of the pot" looks small `[OPINION]` ([Fitzgerald, Card Player 2025](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/sam-greenwood-shares-his-punt-of-the-day-strategy/articles/card-player-magazine-38-11-strategy-10-mistakes-low-stakes-players-fitzgerald)). Players also check back strong river hands because they could not fold to a raise `[OPINION]` ([Hanson, Card Player 2014](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66279-vanessa-selbst-27-14/articles/21967-crushing-live-poker-with-twitter)). Selbrede's donk-bet article gives no live donk frequency at all ([Selbrede 2017c](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/donk-betting-in-small-stakes-live-no-limit-hold-em-28638.htm)).

### Bet size tracks hand strength, by opinion only, and one tell is disputed

The sizing map comes mainly from Bart Hanson and Ed Miller, with no hit rates. Players "usually" size by hand strength: larger with strong one-pair hands to "protect", two pair "blasts the pot" on connected boards, very strong hands may be slow-played or bet small; Hanson calls this "one of the most consistent patterns", from hands at $5/$10 that he generalises to "mid and low" stakes `[OPINION]` ([Hanson, Card Player 2014](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1037161)). A small continuation bet is "usually indicative of weaker hands" such as 99, TT, AK, AQ, unless it is a monster; strong players keep c-bets around half to two-thirds pot so size reveals nothing `[OPINION]` ([Hanson, Card Player 2012](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66214-haig-kelegian-25-20/articles/20863-crushing-live-poker-with-twitter)). Small flop raises, especially on wet boards, "are almost always indicative of top pairish types of hands" while two pair or better "will raise big" `[OPINION]` ([Hanson, Crush Live Poker 2012](https://crushlivepoker.com/articles/small-flop-rasies)). Small out-of-position river leads are block bets with medium showdown hands, sized from about one-tenth to one-fifth of the pot, and "many players" block and then still call a raise `[OPINION]` ([Hanson, Crush Live Poker 2014](https://crushlivepoker.com/articles/countering-blockish-bets); [Card Player 2014](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1037112)). Miller adds three patterns from $2/$5: a small bet followed by a much larger one is strong value; a one-third-pot bet into several players on a draw-heavy board is a medium hand or draw (strong hands bet $50-$70 into $107); and a shrinking turn size followed by a river check is one pair playing defensively `[OPINION]` ([Miller, Card Player 2016](https://cardplayer.com/poker-news/20644-poker-strategy-with-ed-miller-three-bet-sizing-tells)). A nit's bet of a third or more of his stack is "a huge hand — every time" `[OPINION]` ([Miller, Card Player 2011](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65882-eugene-katchalov-24-6/articles/20055-beating-the-locals-in-vegas)).

**What an overbet from a weak player means is disputed.** Hanson, Daniel Marks and Fitzgerald say strength: overbets are strong "nine times out of 10" and the pool never overbet-bluffs `[OPINION]` ([Fitzgerald, Card Player 2025](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/sam-greenwood-shares-his-punt-of-the-day-strategy/articles/card-player-magazine-38-11-strategy-10-mistakes-low-stakes-players-fitzgerald)). Jerry Fang, in the same panel as Marks, says most live overbets are panic plays by non-thinking players trying to scare opponents out `[OPINION]` ([Tapscott panel, Card Player 2013](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66253-mike-watson-26-21/articles/21475-head-games-cash-game-overbetting-dynamics)). The small-bet tell is two-sided by Hanson's own description (weak blocker or trapped monster), which makes it the least reliable of the three. No dataset links live bet size to shown-down hand strength; one content site's "80%+" reliability figure for amateur sizing tells has no method `[UNSOURCED NUMBER]` (snippet) ([DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/live-poker-tells-physical-reads-guide)).

### Showdown behaviour: no live statistics at all

No live low-stakes WTSD, W$SD, river call frequency by size, or bluff-catch success rate was found. Opinion says players "want to get to showdown", size their own bets down to ensure it, and think in absolute terms (a hand "is not a $200 hand") `[OPINION]` ([Hanson, Card Player 2014](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1037112)). The only numeric benchmarks are online (regulars' WTSD usually 27-32%; station marker above 32%) `[DATA-online]` (snippet) ([Hand2Note stats guide](https://hand2note.com/Blog/Features/essential-postflop-stats); [DeucesCracked HUD guide](https://www.deucescracked.com/poker/strategy/hud-stats)).

## A2. Archetypes: only the loose-passive limper rests on tracked data; the rest are opinion bands

### No census of types exists, so table composition is an assumption

No source gives a percentage split by archetype for any live low-stakes pool. What is supported: the pool average of 37/6 cannot come from a table of mostly tight players, so loose-passive is the modal type `[CALC on DATA-live]` ([Selbrede 2017a](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/donkey-poker-low-stakes-live-games-differ-online-27134.htm)); 90% of streamed $2/$5-$5/$10 tables had at least two weak players and 57% had three to five `[DATA-live, other pool]` ([Hand2Note](https://hand2note.com/Blog/Databases/online-vs-live-poker-insights)); six hand-tracked low-limit regulars had VPIPs of 14, about 14, 18, 27, 30 and 36%, and the one labelled a "nit" by impression was the 36% `[DATA-live, single observer]` ([Kat Martin, Red Chip](https://redchippoker.com/know-thine-enemy-profiling-your-opponents-for-poker-profit/)). Fitzgerald's prevalence labels are the only other guide: over-callers "common", over-folders about one in 10-20, true maniacs rare (the label is wrong "nine times out of 10"), 20-40% of players capable of a double-barrel bluff `[OPINION]` ([Card Player 2025](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/sam-greenwood-shares-his-punt-of-the-day-strategy/articles/card-player-magazine-38-11-strategy-10-mistakes-low-stakes-players-fitzgerald); [Fitzgerald](https://jonathanlittlepoker.com/exploitativealex/)). Miller places nits as "common in small-stakes games, especially daytime games" and pros and "wannabes" mostly at $2/$5 and $5/$10 `[OPINION]` ([Card Player 2011](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65882-eugene-katchalov-24-6/articles/20055-beating-the-locals-in-vegas)). The supportable statement for a 9-seat table is "at least two, commonly three to five weak players"; any finer split is a tuning choice.

Published stat bands disagree, and the format behind each band explains part of it. Jonathan Little gives nit 8-15/5-12, TAG 15-25/12-20, LAG 25-40/20-35, maniac 50+/35+, calling station 35-50/5-10, GTO-oriented 22-28/18-24 (VPIP/PFR) `[OPINION, format not stated]` ([Little, PokerCoaching](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/different-poker-players/)). PokerSkill gives nit <18/<14, TAG 22-27/18-23, LAG 28-38/22-30, maniac 45+/35+, station 35-60/<12 `[OPINION, no sources, likely 6-max]` ([PokerSkill](https://www.pokerskill.com/blog/vpip-and-pfr/)). A HUD guide gives example values 14/12 nit, 22/19 TAG, 35/8 station with aggression factor below 1.5 and WTSD above 32% for stations and aggression factor above 4.0 for hyper-aggression `[DATA-online framing, uncited]` ([DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/poker/strategy/hud-stats)). The lower bands suit full ring; the higher ones reflect 6-max or deep straddled games. That reading is the research team's, not a sourced statement.

### Older tight-passive nit ("old man coffee")

The nit is defined as an older low-stakes player with an "extremely tight range" `[OPINION]` ([PokerNews 2026](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/where-did-the-term-old-man-coffee-come-from-52735.htm)). Tracked tight regulars sit at 14-18% VPIP; the tightest range one observer has seen is about 6% (AA-99, AKs-ATs, KQs, AKo-AQo) from regulars who "fork the range so that the weaker hands are limps"; Ed Miller's published nit range of about 21% (AA-22, all suited aces, KQs-KTs, QJs-54s, QTs, AKo-ATo, KQo-KTo, QJo-JTo) is called too loose by that observer; older players with a limit hold'em background undervalue suitedness, and "symmetrising" a 21% range (pairing each suited hand with its offsuit partner) lifts it to about 35% `[DATA-live, single observer, plus OPINION]` ([Kat Martin, Red Chip](https://redchippoker.com/know-thine-enemy-profiling-your-opponents-for-poker-profit/)). Nits limp strong hands (AKo, KK, QQ) and limp-reraise more than other limpers `[OPINION]` ([Brokos, GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-to-handle-loose-passive-limpers/)); they oversize preflop with medium-strong hands such as JJ, which reveals the hand `[OPINION]` ([Miller 2011](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65882-eugene-katchalov-24-6/articles/20055-beating-the-locals-in-vegas)). The 3-bet range is value-only; the single numeric example is 2% over 2,000 hands, from an online micro-stakes article `[OPINION, online example]` ([BlackRain79 guest article](https://www.blackrain79.com/2019/09/play-gto-against-bad-poker-players.html)). Postflop, Miller's tight-player traits are limp-folding, bet-folding, pot-controlling, refusing to fire a second or third barrel and "refusing to felt without the nuts" `[OPINION, chapter titles only]` ([Miller, book contents](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/2572519-playing-the-player-preorder-details-and-table-of-contents?tab=book)); a nit needs "more than a pair" to call three bets `[OPINION]` ([Fitzgerald, PokerNews 2023](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/alex-fitzgerald-nits-stations-and-maniacs-43259.htm)); he bluffs only when hero has shown so much weakness that a fold is nearly certain `[OPINION]` ([Miller 2011](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65882-eugene-katchalov-24-6/articles/20055-beating-the-locals-in-vegas)); and a raise from a player who has not raised for an hour is the nuts or near it `[ANECDOTE]` ([Hanson, Card Player 2012](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1036018)). Stack behaviour: older regulars "buy in for $100 and just keep announcing they're adding on" `[OPINION, forum]` ([PokerChipForum](https://pokerchipforum.com/threads/scared-to-take-the-leap-starting-1-3.129852)). Best adjustments: steal his blinds, fold good hands to his aggression, attack his big blind with multiple bets, do not bluff-catch `[OPINION]` ([Little](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/different-poker-players/); [Fitzgerald](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/alex-fitzgerald-nits-stations-and-maniacs-43259.htm)); call his small flop check-raise with top pair good kicker rather than re-raising, then value-bet the river when checked to `[OPINION]` ([Hanson](https://crushlivepoker.com/articles/small-flop-rasies)). A named variant, the "Sticky Bun", is tight preflop but "has a very hard time folding to action after the flop" `[OPINION]` ([Ashley Adams, PokerNews 2017](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/player-profiling-four-new-poker-archetypes-28747.htm)). **Gaps:** c-bet frequency, fold frequencies by street and size, share of table, tilt behaviour.

### Loose-passive calling station / recreational limper

This is the only archetype with tracked numbers: modelled as 35/6, limping roughly the 6th-35th percentile of hands from every seat, calling an isolation raise about 56% of the time, and calling raises nearly twice as often as online players `[DATA-live / MODEL]` ([Selbrede 2017d](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/preflop-raising-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-cash-games-29209.htm)). Opinion bands run wider: 35-50/5-10 `[OPINION]` ([Little](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/different-poker-players/)), up to about 60% VPIP `[OPINION]` ([Sweeney, Red Chip](https://redchippoker.com/using-a-hud-for-live-poker/)). The limp range is weak-to-medium Ax, offsuit broadways, suited and offsuit connectors and random suited high cards (K7s, Q8s, J7s); pure trash limps are "rare these days" `[OPINION]` ([Upswing 2025](https://upswingpoker.com/limps-poker-open-limpers-strategy/)). Low-limit players "include all aces" and all pairs, and when they show down junk "the vast majority of the time the junk hands were being folded postflop" `[DATA-live, single observer]` ([Kat Martin](https://redchippoker.com/know-thine-enemy-profiling-your-opponents-for-poker-profit/)). Re-raises are rare and premium (see A1).

**Postflop the sources describe two different response curves under the same label.** The true station is inelastic: "calling stations do not reduce their calling frequency when you bet bigger" and call with second pair and worse `[OPINION]` ([Little](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/different-poker-players/)); Miller's bad-player traits are "peeling light on the flop and getting sticky at showdown" and "absolutely refusing to fold an overpair" `[OPINION, chapter titles]` ([Miller, book contents](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/2572519-playing-the-player-preorder-details-and-table-of-contents?tab=book)). The average recreational player is elastic: $1/$2 players "call wide against small bets, but they hate getting stacked", fold "far more than GTO would allow" to a large bet, and rarely call a stack with one pair `[OPINION]` ([Little 2026](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/5-tips-to-beat-small-stakes-cash-games-in-2026/)); limpers chase draws yet "fold more than they should on scare cards" `[OPINION]` ([Upswing 2025](https://upswingpoker.com/limps-poker-open-limpers-strategy/)). The sources support the direction of each curve but not the slope. Both variants bet when strong and check when weak, rarely bluff, and raise only with real hands; when a limper raises, usually on the turn, one pair should fold `[OPINION]` ([Lee Jones](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/3-deadly-techniques-to-punish-limpers-in-live-cash-games/)). Sizing is "by feel", with odd amounts driven by hand strength `[OPINION, online-club source]` ([RP Union](https://rpunion.com/how-to-spot-a-recreational-player.html)). Stacks are commonly $100-$300 at $1/$2-$1/$3, many at the $100 minimum `[OPINION, forum]` ([PokerChipForum](https://pokerchipforum.com/threads/scared-to-take-the-leap-starting-1-3.129852)).

Best adjustments: bet at least 65-75% pot with top pair or better (a coached example found 30% pot used where 70-80% was right), value-bet second pair, never bluff `[OPINION]` ([Little](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/different-poker-players/)); against limpers, value bets of 66-105% pot and frequent double barrels, and with missed hands on dry boards about 30% pot once and then give up `[OPINION]` ([Upswing 2025](https://upswingpoker.com/limps-poker-open-limpers-strategy/)). Solver runs against an assumed station profile refine "never bluff": small, frequent, value-weighted flop bets gain EV (quarter-pot bet used 83% against 71% versus an equilibrium opponent on Q-J-5), turn barrels drop, and river bluffs go to near zero `[SOLVER-SIM]` ([Brokos, GTO Wizard 2026](https://blog.gtowizard.com/exploiting-profiles-episode-i-the-calling-station/)). **Gaps:** every postflop frequency; the slope of call-versus-size; share of stations versus elastic recreationals.

### Loose-aggressive maniac, and the good LAG

Bands for the maniac are VPIP 50+ / PFR 35+ and "rarely" profitable; for the skilled LAG 25-40 / 20-35 `[OPINION]` ([Little](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/different-poker-players/)). Maniacs "constantly spam reraises in preflop" and fire multiple barrels `[OPINION]` ([Fitzgerald, PokerNews 2023](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/alex-fitzgerald-nits-stations-and-maniacs-43259.htm)); a loose hijack raiser may hold "half of all poker hands" `[OPINION]` ([Little 2026](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/5-tips-to-beat-small-stakes-cash-games-in-2026/)). Fitzgerald's limiter matters for table composition: true over-bluffers are rare, and most players labelled maniacs are loud 3-bettors and floaters who still usually have it in big pots `[OPINION]` ([Fitzgerald, Card Player 2025](https://www.cardplayer.com/poker-news/poker-strategy-mistakes-low-stakes-players-exploit-them)). Forum posters split maniacs into those who fold to aggression and "maniac stations" `[OPINION, forum]` (snippet) ([TwoPlusTwo](https://forumserver.twoplustwo.com/170/live-no-limit-holdem-cash/how-best-play-against-smart-maniac-1812381/)). Related types: the "Chirper", who makes small preflop raises from any seat with a wide weak range `[OPINION]` ([Adams](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/player-profiling-four-new-poker-archetypes-28747.htm)), and Miller's "Playboy" tourist, dressed for a club, who runs wild all-in bluffs, calls down light, and typically sits about an hour before shoving when bored `[OPINION]` ([Miller, Card Player 2011](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65881-aussie-millions-24-5/articles/20032-beating-the-tourists-in-vegas)). Splashy players are the only type Miller considers worth bluff-catching at $1/$2, specifically half-pot river bets after a missed draw `[OPINION]` ([Miller, Card Player 2018](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66390-george-antonakos-wins-cppt-bike-main-event-31-18/articles/23459-bluff-catching-at-1-2)).

**Counter-strategy is disputed in one respect.** The main line is to pick a halfway-decent hand, call down and not raise him off his bluffs `[OPINION]` ([Fitzgerald](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/alex-fitzgerald-nits-stations-and-maniacs-43259.htm)), tighten up, bet and raise bigger, and call in position with top pair or better `[OPINION]` ([Little](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/different-poker-players/)). A forum camp says maniacs "hate getting 3-bet and check-raised", so stay aggressive against the folding kind and "3bet huge" with {55+, AT+, KJ+} against the station kind `[OPINION, forum]` (snippet) ([TwoPlusTwo](https://forumserver.twoplustwo.com/170/live-low-stakes-nl/how-play-against-maniac-1691440/)). Against a good LAG: sit on his left, defend the big blind wider, do not over-fold to his 3-bets `[OPINION]` ([Little](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/different-poker-players/)). **Gaps:** 3-bet percentage, bluff frequency by street, fold to re-raise, stack size, share of table.

### Young solver-style player, and how the style misfires at low stakes

Evidence on this type is the thinnest of the five, and most of it is advice to the reader not to be this player rather than description of an opponent. The band is VPIP 22-28 / PFR 18-24 with a narrow gap, "profitable at higher stakes" `[OPINION]` ([Little](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/different-poker-players/)); men under 45 opened 25%+ of hands from the lojack or later `[DATA-live, author-reported, tournaments likely included]` ([Fitzgerald](https://jonathanlittlepoker.com/exploitativealex/)). The named misapplications, all `[OPINION]`, are these. Thinking players "out level themselves" and give passive opponents credit for bluffs (a young 3-bettor paying off an old passive player's turn check-raise in a $5,000 pot) ([Hanson, Card Player 2012](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1036018)). They make river bluffs and bluff-raises against players who cannot fold top pair, defend a fixed share of range against players who 3-bet 2%, and call at minimum-defence frequency against overbets from passive players ([BlackRain79 guest article](https://www.blackrain79.com/2019/09/play-gto-against-bad-poker-players.html)). They treat a loose opener as if "they have a proper hijack range" ([Little 2026](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/5-tips-to-beat-small-stakes-cash-games-in-2026/)). They range-bet or small-bet into multiway fields, where solver work itself says to stop range-betting ([Tombos21, GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/10-tips-multiway-pots-in-poker/)). They defend blinds from rake-free or ante charts: with rake and no ante the big blind defends about 28-29% against a cutoff open versus 76% in a rake-free ante game `[SOLVER, quoted by author]` ([Little, Card Player 2026](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1633660)). Goone's stronger claim is that board-texture and range-advantage reasoning is "irrelevant in live poker" and that he has needed a theory-first approach against only two players in six years `[OPINION]` ([Goone, Card Player 2024](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66553-tyler-ninja-blevins-37-23/articles/25224-marc-goone-explains-how-to-properly-size-your-bets)). Best adjustments: against a genuinely balanced player stay near balanced and exploit only observed deviations ([Little](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/different-poker-players/)); 3-bet frequent late-position openers in position, which profits "even with mistakes" `[DATA-live, author-reported]` ([Fitzgerald](https://jonathanlittlepoker.com/exploitativealex/)). **Gaps:** no source quantifies how often this type over-bluffs, under-folds or small-bets multiway, and none gives a counter-strategy specific to a solver-style opponent who is misapplying theory.

### Competent tight-aggressive regular

Bands: 15-25 / 12-20 `[OPINION]` ([Little](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/different-poker-players/)); about 15% VPIP as an online full-ring reference `[OPINION]` ([Sweeney](https://redchippoker.com/using-a-hud-for-live-poker/)); Selbrede's recommended live VPIP of 18-20% overall, about 10% early and 30% on the button `[MODEL/recommendation]` ([Selbrede 2017b](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/donkey-poker-are-you-a-position-dummy-27256.htm)). The tracked figure for live winners, 31/20 with a 13% 3-bet, aggression factor 1.6 and 40% won-when-saw-flop, is looser than any coach band and comes from deep, straddled $2/$5+ streamed games `[DATA-live, other pool]` ([Hand2Note](https://hand2note.com/Blog/Databases/online-vs-live-poker-insights)); for a casino $1/$2-$3/$5 regular the coach band is the better starting point, with Hand2Note as evidence that good regulars loosen when the table is soft (research-team reading). Miller's prescription for the type is pairs, suited big cards and suited connectors, never limping outside the blinds, being the tightest player and the most frequent 3-bettor `[OPINION]` ([Miller, Card Player 2015](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66294-cppt-foxwoods-28-2/articles/22227-three-skills-to-beat-1-2)).

The regular's documented leaks are the most useful part. Miller on $2/$5 regulars: too many hands preflop, getting all-in on the flop too easily with strong-but-vulnerable hands, folding too often to small flop and turn bets, and checking too many hands to showdown `[OPINION]` ([Miller, Red Chip](https://redchippoker.com/how-live-regs-shoot-themselves-in-the-foot/amp/)). Goone's informal test of six live pros winning 8-9bb/hour: facing a c-bet they raised too many strong hands, called too many weak ones and under-check-raised bluffs `[DATA-live, author-reported, six players]` ([Goone](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66553-tyler-ninja-blevins-37-23/articles/25224-marc-goone-explains-how-to-properly-size-your-bets)). Solid players who are tight preflop and fold flops are the least likely to bluff rivers, and "don't pay them off" works more reliably at $2/$5 than $1/$2 `[OPINION]` ([Miller 2018](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66390-george-antonakos-wins-cppt-bike-main-event-31-18/articles/23459-bluff-catching-at-1-2)). Galfond describes $2/$5 regulars who under-bluff rivers and, having learned to over-fold, also call too little `[OPINION]` ([Galfond 2023](https://www.philgalfond.com/articles/how-understanding-your-player-pool-can-boost-your-winrate)). Best adjustments: avoid big pots without a strong hand, attack capped ranges, do not bluff-catch anyone who plays solidly, barrel with small bets, and bluff rivers large against identified over-folders. Two weak-tight variants are named: the "Roly Poly", who raises light preflop and "will roll up and go away in the face of any action" when he misses (float him; a small donk usually wins), and the "Butterfly", a solid player who floats flops to take pots on the turn (bet medium hands less out of position, re-raise turns and rivers more) `[OPINION]` ([Adams](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/player-profiling-four-new-poker-archetypes-28747.htm)). **Gaps:** c-bet and fold frequencies; number of regulars per table.

### Other types the sources identify

Miller's Las Vegas taxonomy adds three tourists and a local `[OPINION]`. The "first-timer" does not know procedures, is impatient, calls all-ins with ace-high and "almost never leaves the table with chips". The "timid tourist" knows the rules but is visibly nervous, limps, bets too small, checks back too often, rarely bluffs, and over-folds (even sets) to pressure. The "casual local" plays once or twice a week, is too loose on every street, limps weak hands, calls big raises and under-bets good hands ([Miller, tourists](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65881-aussie-millions-24-5/articles/20032-beating-the-tourists-in-vegas); [Miller, locals](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65882-eugene-katchalov-24-6/articles/20055-beating-the-locals-in-vegas)). The short-stacked minimum buyer is supported only as "minimum buy-in is common" and "an instant target" `[OPINION, forum]` ([PokerChipForum](https://pokerchipforum.com/threads/scared-to-take-the-leap-starting-1-3.129852)); nothing describes how that player's strategy differs. The "sticky Asian gambler" stereotype was not described in any source retrieved; Welch and Hwang mention region and ethnicity as a claimed cue for aggression, and Hwang's own example is a misread ([Hwang, Card Player 2009](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65764-barry-shulman-22-22/articles/18846-profiling-errors)).

### Tilt, stuck, winning, late night: pool-level direction only, nothing per archetype

No source describes how any specific archetype changes when tilted, stuck or winning. The only quantitative evidence is from high-stakes online play: among 346 experienced players at $25/$50, after a $1,000 loss in one hand 135 played looser than after a big win and 68 did the reverse at six-handed tables, and those who loosened did worse than their norm `[DATA-online, high stakes]` ([Smith, Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/au/blog/what-the-luck/201612/make-peace-your-losses); [Smith, Levere and Kurtzman 2009](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/mnsc.1090.1044)). That is roughly two in three loosening, not all; transfer to $1/$2 live recreationals is assumed. Experienced players are less affected by emotional priming than inexperienced ones `[DATA, lab, sample sizes not given in the secondary source]` ([Card Player](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66289-shan-jing-27-23/articles/22139-what-scientists-have-learned-from-studying-poker-players)). Live opinion agrees on direction: recreational players "play very badly when they are behind in a session" and stay late chasing; players who must leave soon "blow up" at the end of their session and call more, so value-bet them more and bluff them less `[OPINION]` ([Hanson, Card Player](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66292-mark-newhouse-27-26/articles/22180-crushing-live-poker-with-twitter)); the first thing to disappear on tilt is folding `[OPINION, no sources]` ([PokerSkill](https://www.pokerskill.com/blog/poker-tilt/)). Nothing was found on how recreational players play when winning, on hit-and-run behaviour, or on the measured effect of alcohol or fatigue.

**On time of day the sources partly conflict.** Hanson says night games are better than day games (younger opponents, more alcohol, a gambling mood toward early morning) and daytime regulars are often retirees ([Hanson](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66292-mark-newhouse-27-26/articles/22180-crushing-live-poker-with-twitter)); Upswing says "late nights and weekends are softest" ([Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/live-poker-tips-strategy/)); Dalla describes graveyard-shift opponents as "tired, on tilt, playing to get even" ([Dalla, Card Player 2006](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/10-the-bicycle-casino-14-15/articles/252-tales-from-the-felt-playing-poker-around-the-clock)). All are `[OPINION]`. Against that, the only tracked source found variation in average VPIP by location and time "not generally dramatic" across four Vegas rooms `[DATA-live]` ([Selbrede 2017](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/donkey-poker-do-you-play-too-many-hands-27182.htm)), and a forum excerpt suggests weekday daytime "might be softer" (snippet) ([PokerForum.ca](https://pokerforum.ca/discussion/13813/changes-in-game-and-players-depending-on-time-of-day-and-day-of-week)). Both can hold if night-time softness comes from stack depth, alcohol and stuck players rather than average preflop looseness; that is inference. A defensible implementation is a session-level modifier (late night and weekend raise the share of recreational, stuck and loosened players; weekday daytime raises the share of retired nits) with drift applied probabilistically per player and more strongly to recreational profiles.

## A3. Reading players: appearance is an opinion-based prior; 30 hands separates extremes, 200-500 separates neighbours

### Which cues are claimed to predict type, and how reliable

No dataset measures how well any appearance cue predicts playing style at a live table. Every source that endorses appearance-based priors also reports a personal misread. The stance of the named authors, all `[OPINION]`: stereotypes are "a starting point", most small-stakes players "will conform to the stereotypes", but "it only takes one outlier", and a contradicting showdown should end the stereotype completely ([Welch, PokerNews 2016](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/be-cautious-when-using-stereotypes-in-poker-25425.htm)); the read concerns the average player of a type, not the individual ([Galfond](https://philgalfond.com/articles/the-anatomy-of-a-read)); the primacy effect makes players overweight first impressions ([Hwang, Card Player 2009](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65764-barry-shulman-22-22/articles/18846-profiling-errors)); the right default for an unknown $1/$2-$1/$3 player is tight, unlikely to bluff big, big bets respected "until I learn differently" ([Elwood](https://zacharyelwood.substack.com/p/some-thoughts-on-quick-reads-of-opponent)).

The cues fall on two axes of different strength (the tiering is the research team's; no source gives numbers). Cues for regular-versus-recreational are stated confidently and are mechanically plausible; cues for tight-versus-loose or passive-versus-aggressive are hedged and each comes with a published misread. "Looks like a regular" does not pick a style: Hull and Miller stress that many self-described low-stakes pros are not long-term winners, and Elwood reads a neat stack and riffling as seriousness "regardless of if he was good or not".

### First-orbit actions outrank appearance

Open-limping, especially limp-calling, is the most consistently named early marker of a recreational loose-passive player; sizing "by feel" is second; a raise from a habitual limper is treated as strong. "Open-limping is the clearest single signal" and two limps in one orbit is enough to start adjusting `[OPINION, online-club source]` ([RP Union](https://rpunion.com/how-to-spot-a-recreational-player.html)); four questions (does he open-limp, 3-bet non-premium hands, check-raise flops, fold his big blind to steals) are claimed to give "80% of the practical value" of a full stat readout within about thirty hands `[UNSOURCED NUMBER, online context]` ([DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-player-profiling-notes-live-online-guide)). A counter-view holds that open-limping is less diagnostic in low-stakes live games than online `[OPINION]` (snippet) ([PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/a-preflop-question-is-limping-lame-27755.htm)). Limping separates loose-passive recreationals from everyone else; it does not separate a nit from a competent regular. Showdowns are the strongest update `[OPINION]` ([Welch](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/be-cautious-when-using-stereotypes-in-poker-25425.htm)). No source ranks first-orbit actions by measured predictive power, and sourcing on min-raises, donk bets and straddling as diagnostics is thin or contradictory.

### Documented mismatches run mostly one way

No source gives a rate at which appearance misleads. The published first-person misreads are: an older wealthy businessman typed as tight who was bluffing (Galfond calls it "a bad fold against him specifically" while defending the read against the average) `[ANECDOTE]` ([Galfond](https://philgalfond.com/articles/the-anatomy-of-a-read)); an older man typed as tight who was bluffing, and a grandmotherly woman who 3-bet and called all-in with 6-4 offsuit `[ANECDOTE]` ([Welch](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/be-cautious-when-using-stereotypes-in-poker-25425.htm)); and an apparent "action gambler" whose first few hands seemed to confirm the label but who in fact raised mostly from late position and played well postflop `[ANECDOTE]` ([Hwang](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65764-barry-shulman-22-22/articles/18846-profiling-errors)). Add the single-observer finding that a regular labelled a nit by impression was 36% VPIP `[DATA-live, single observer]` ([Kat Martin](https://redchippoker.com/know-thine-enemy-profiling-your-opponents-for-poker-profit/)). The costly error is over-folding to someone who looks tight; Hwang's case (misleading appearance plus a short confirming run) is the design pattern worth reproducing. No published example was found for "young hoodie player who is a calling station" or "drunk who is a solid regular".

### How many hands it takes

Published guidance is rule-of-thumb and spans an order of magnitude: VPIP and PFR after about 20 hands, aggression factor and 3-bet after 100, fold-to-c-bet 100, turn stats 300, river stats 1,000 `[OPINION, online HUD context]` ([BlackRain79](https://www.blackrain79.com/2017/11/poker-hud-stat-sample-size.html)); preflop tendencies in 30-50 hands "or about an hour live", postflop frequencies after at least 200 hands, at least three consistent observations before acting, a live sizing pattern "usually emerges within two hours" `[OPINION, unsourced]` ([DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-player-profiling-notes-live-online-guide)); classification "within two orbits" from posture, chips, buy-in and first showdowns `[OPINION, anonymous]` ([BetterPoker](https://betterpoker.vercel.app/learn/live-poker/live-dynamics/live-player-census)). Selbrede says a few hundred hands suffice for a reliable pool-average VPIP `[OPINION]` ([Selbrede 2017](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/donkey-poker-do-you-play-too-many-hands-27182.htm)). No statistical treatment specific to live reads exists in the poker literature found.

The following binomial figures are the research team's own calculation `[CALC]` (independent trials per hand; real hands are not independent, so true uncertainty is larger). They show worst-case misclassification when choosing between two candidate types with the best single cut-off.

One orbit is close to worthless for VPIP alone; one hour (about 30 hands) reliably separates "plays almost everything" from "plays almost nothing"; separating a TAG from a slightly loose regular takes multiple sessions. Showdowns arrive much more slowly: a player entering 15-45% of 25-30 hands per hour plays roughly 4-13 hands per hour and shows down a fraction of those, and no sourced live showdown rate exists, so showdowns-per-hour is an assumption. Sources treat one inconsistent showdown as enough to discard a stereotype (Welch) but require about three consistent observations to attribute an aggressive tendency (DeucesCracked); no source quantifies how far one showdown should move a read.

### Physical and timing tells: a tiebreaker, strongest against recreational players

Authors agree that tells are a layer on top of betting patterns, that they work mainly on recreational players, and that because passive low-stakes players rarely make big bluffs the useful tells are waiting-for-action tells rather than "is he bluffing" tells `[OPINION]` ([Elwood 2012](https://www.readingpokertells.com/2012/11/poker-tells-at-live-1-2-no-limit/)). No independently published hit rate exists for any tell in live cash play. ## A4. Winning strategy: tight raise-or-fold preflop, big thin value, deep respect for raises, and few bluffs outside capped spots

### Consensus baseline preflop

The consensus is "tight, raise-or-fold, bigger sizes than online", and it is almost entirely opinion. Miller's first and most important skill for $1/$2 is a simple static preflop strategy: mostly pocket pairs, suited big cards and suited connectors, frequent raising, frequent re-raising, and "never limp from any position outside the blinds" `[OPINION]` ([Miller, Card Player 2015](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66294-cppt-foxwoods-28-2/articles/22227-three-skills-to-beat-1-2)). **No accessible source gives concrete position-by-position opening ranges for live 8-10 handed raked games**; the published charts (Miller's "The Course", Upswing's live charts, Little's book) are paywalled or images, and the generic full-ring percentages seen in snippets (UTG 9-16%, CO 26-32%, BTN 40-50%) come from low-quality sites `[OPINION, not live-specific]` (snippet) ([PokerSkill charts](https://www.pokerskill.com/charts/opening-ranges/)). Selbrede's 18-20% overall, 10% early, 30% button is the only live-specific figure `[MODEL/recommendation]` ([Selbrede 2017b](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/donkey-poker-are-you-a-position-dummy-27256.htm)).

Open sizing clusters at 4bb plus 1bb per limper, with a range from "3-4x plus one" in position (Lee Jones) to "5x plus one" (Sweeney) and an instruction to keep adding 1bb "until those players start folding" `[OPINION]` ([Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/vs-multiple-limpers/); [Sweeney, Red Chip](https://redchippoker.com/raising-preflop-limp-fest-pots/); [Lee Jones](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/3-deadly-techniques-to-punish-limpers-in-live-cash-games/)). The solver view cuts the other way against competent defenders: in a 10%-rake, 2bb-cap, no-flop-no-drop solve at 125bb, AA earns 11.02bb opening 3x and 10.34bb opening 2x, but about 2bb less at 7x than at 2x; the author concludes larger raises "may be good exploits from late position against loose, passive opponents (i.e., most live cash games)" `[SOLVER]` ([Brokos, GTO Wizard 2025](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-to-respond-to-large-preflop-raises-in-poker/)). The verdict on size therefore depends on how elastic the modelled callers are, and Selbrede's inelastic-calling premise is an assumption, not a measurement. An open between roughly 4bb and 7bb first-in should not be flagged on size alone (research-team reading of the spread).

**Limping is a real disagreement.** Nobody defends open-limping or limp-calling: Little calls limp-calling a raise "one of the costliest habits" `[OPINION]` ([Little, PokerCoaching 2024](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/limping-in-poker/)). Over-limping in late position behind two or more limpers with small pairs, suited connectors (54s-98s) and small suited aces is endorsed by Little, Upswing, Sweeney and Roy Cooke `[OPINION]` ([Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/vs-multiple-limpers/); [Cooke, Card Player](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66342-jason-koon-29-24/articles/22924-learning-no-limit-from-scratch-playing-sets-and-set-mining-correctly-the-basics)) and opposed by Miller, who values simplicity. The one data-based input: with 22 on the button behind one limper, immediate EV is +$0.63 raising to $10 against −$0.38 limping, and raising beat limping for almost every hand except 63s, 53s, 65o, 54o; but JTo facing three limpers is −$1.23 to raise against −$0.25 to limp `[MODEL on DATA-live]` ([Selbrede 2017d](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/preflop-raising-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-cash-games-29209.htm); [Selbrede 2018](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/implied-value-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-games-29726.htm)).

Rake pushes toward 3-bet-or-fold and tight blind defence. In 200bb 6-max solves, players facing a lojack raise enter 25-35% fewer hands with rake than without, mostly by dropping calls, and the big blind calls a min-raise "barely 60% as often" `[SOLVER, online structure]` ([Brokos, GTO Wizard 2024](https://blog.gtowizard.com/preflop-raise-sizing-examining-2-key-factors/)); with a 10% rake and 2bb cap at 125bb the button facing a 4x open "folds virtually all hands with a card below a Ten, and even AJo" `[SOLVER]` ([Brokos 2025](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-to-respond-to-large-preflop-raises-in-poker/)). These are 6-max online structures; the magnitudes are directional for 9-handed live games with a 1-2.5bb cap. Godshall adds that 3-betting more of the continuing range is right because pots that end preflop pay no rake `[OPINION + SOLVER charts]` ([Upswing 2022](https://upswingpoker.com/live-preflop-rake-adjustments/)).

### Isolation raising

Sizing formulas for live play: 4bb + 1 per limper (Upswing, Selbrede); add 1bb out of position (Little); 8x or more from the blinds over three limpers, e.g. $25-$35 at $2/$3 (Lee Jones); 5x + 1 per limper, with $20-$30 usually best in $1/$2 limp-fests because "$10 invites everyone and $40 gets no action" (Sweeney) `[OPINION]` ([Lee Jones](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/3-deadly-techniques-to-punish-limpers-in-live-cash-games/); [Sweeney](https://redchippoker.com/raising-preflop-limp-fest-pots/)). Ranges tighten with each limper: the immediately profitable button range falls from about 33% of hands against one limper to 13.4% against four, and the big blind can raise 27.6% (22+, A2+, KT+, QJ) against one early limper `[MODEL on DATA-live]` ([Selbrede 2017d](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/preflop-raising-in-live-1-2-no-limit-hold-em-cash-games-29209.htm)); Sweeney's limp-fest default is TT+ and AQ+, adding AJ, KQ, 99 `[OPINION]` ([Sweeney](https://redchippoker.com/raising-preflop-limp-fest-pots/)). Upswing's live tips say to "play looser against most limpers" in practice, which conflicts with Sweeney's strong-weighted stance `[OPINION]` ([Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/live-poker-tips-strategy/)). Isolation usually fails to isolate: a 5bb raise over one limper is heads-up only 47% of the time and three- or four-way about 30% (table in A1), and Sweeney says to "expect a multiway pot even after you raise". Iso hands should therefore be graded on multiway playability. No source gives live iso ranges by position and limper count as hand lists, nor any data on how calling frequency changes with iso size.

### Multiway pots

Solver and coach sources point the same way: tighter value thresholds, far fewer bluffs, smaller bets, more checking, nut potential over raw equity, and near-total respect for raises. Hard numbers exist only for three-way pots with competent ranges. With a lojack open called by both blinds (100bb, 8-max, chip-EV), the raiser checks 11 points more often than heads-up and pot-sized c-bets fall from 18% to 1.3% `[SOLVER]` ([JonnyLaw, GTO Wizard 2026](https://blog.gtowizard.com/playing-in-position-against-two-callers/)). Facing a quarter-pot c-bet on T-7-4 two-tone, the middle player goes from about 10/73/17 fold/call/raise heads-up to 31/39/30 with a player behind, and the player closing the action folds about 45% after a call but 85% after a raise `[SOLVER, MTT about 40bb]` ([Brokos, GTO Wizard 2026](https://blog.gtowizard.com/monkey-in-the-middle-3-way-pot-heuristics/)). The heuristics "almost never bluff without solid drawing equity, except on the river" and "stop range betting" are solver-derived from online 6-max `[SOLVER]` ([Tombos21, GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/10-tips-multiway-pots-in-poker/)). Live coaches add that top pair "is often worth only one street of value and may be a fold against a big bet plus a call or raise" `[OPINION]` ([Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/vs-multiple-limpers/)), and that betting to "push opponents out" misvalues the hand `[OPINION]` ([Miller 2015](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66294-cppt-foxwoods-28-2/articles/22227-three-skills-to-beat-1-2)).

Per-opponent fold needed for a zero-equity bluff to break even, assuming independent equal folding `[CALC]`: one-third pot needs 50% from each of two opponents, 63% from each of three, 71% from each of four; half pot needs 58% / 69% / 76%; pot needs 71% / 79% / 84%. Since a typical range flops a made hand or draw about 63% of the time `[OPINION/MATH]` ([Upswing](https://upswingpoker.com/splashy-live-games/)), a pure bluff into three such opponents works about 5% of the time. A no-equity c-bet into three or more live opponents is an error by default. **No solver or data work on four-way or five-way postflop play exists** (public tools stop at three players postflop, and multiway equilibria are not guaranteed unexploitable), so every magnitude for the modal live flop of four players is untested.

Stack-to-pot planning sits on Red Chip rules of thumb: at SPR 2 or below, top pair and overpairs commit by default; 3-6 is a judgement zone; above 6 one pair is not a stack-off hand `[OPINION]` ([Sweeney, Red Chip](https://redchippoker.com/spr-stack-to-pot-ratio-podcast/)). Miller dissents on flop stack-offs with strong-but-vulnerable hands "unless you hold a set" `[OPINION]` ([Miller, Red Chip](https://redchippoker.com/how-live-regs-shoot-themselves-in-the-foot/amp/)). At $1/$2 with $200 stacks and a $10 open, a heads-up pot has SPR about 8, three-way about 5.8, five-way about 3.7 `[CALC]`, so ordinary multiway raised pots land in the grey zone where live sources say one pair folds to real aggression.

### Value sizing versus stations and nits

The consensus exploit is to bet bigger and thinner because calling ranges are wide and comparatively size-insensitive, and to bet-fold rivers because raises are almost never bluffs. The cleanest sourced test for "missed thin value" is Hanson's: a river value bet is profitable if good "just over 50 percent of the time" when called, and river value bets are "almost like freerolls" because the pool so rarely bluff-raises `[OPINION]` ([Hanson, Card Player 2014](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66279-vanessa-selbst-27-14/articles/21967-crushing-live-poker-with-twitter)). "Check-calling on the river is generally bad in live games" `[OPINION]` ([Hanson, Card Player 2012](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66201-amarillo-slim-25-11/articles/20633-tweet-crushing-live-poker-with-twitter)). Against stations: 65-75%+ pot with top pair or better, 66-105% against limpers, value down to second pair (A2). Against nits and tight straightforward players: keep the pot small with marginal made hands, bet about one-third pot or check when checked to, and fold to continued betting `[OPINION]` ([Little, Card Player 2017](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1038047)). Miller frames size as a lever: with showdown-value hands, a tiny bet gets calls from worse and a 3x-pot bet gets folds from better `[OPINION]` ([Miller, Red Chip](https://redchippoker.com/how-live-regs-shoot-themselves-in-the-foot/amp/)).

**Whether to overbet stations for value is disputed.** Coaches say river overbets exploit players who hero-call with two pair, sets and straights; a 2x-pot bet needs to be called only about a third as often as a two-thirds-pot bet `[OPINION]` ([Tapscott panel, Card Player](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66253-mike-watson-26-21/articles/21475-head-games-cash-game-overbetting-dynamics)). The same panel also holds that opponents "will usually not call an overbet, especially in live games, unless their hand strength is very high" (snippet). A solver run against a station profile that also slow-plays uses no river overbets and bets JJ/AA smaller `[SOLVER-SIM]` ([Brokos, GTO Wizard 2026](https://blog.gtowizard.com/exploiting-profiles-episode-i-the-calling-station/)). The deciding variable is whether the station traps. A second dispute concerns betting or checking strong hands out of position: Goone checks nearly everything because opponents over-stab `[DATA-live, author-reported]`, while a solver run shows that cutting the opponent's stab frequency by only 5% raises the overpair betting frequency by almost 20% `[SOLVER-SIM]` ([Vanja, GTO Wizard 2025](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the_3_biggest_leaks_killing_your_winrate/)). The deciding variable is stab frequency when checked to.

### What bluffing survives

Endorsed: small heads-up c-bets in position ("if you get a fold more than 25 percent of the time, you're gold" at one-third pot) and turn barrels against regulars who give up after calling the flop `[MATH + OPINION]` ([Miller, Red Chip](https://redchippoker.com/how-live-regs-shoot-themselves-in-the-foot/amp/)); stabs and barrels after the flop checks through, because recreational players almost never check back strong hands `[DATA-live, author-reported]` ([Goone](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66553-tyler-ninja-blevins-37-23/articles/25224-marc-goone-explains-how-to-properly-size-your-bets)); large river bets into capped ranges held by players who "hate getting stacked" `[OPINION]` ([Little 2026](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/5-tips-to-beat-small-stakes-cash-games-in-2026/)). Dropped: no-equity bluffs into three or more players, multi-barrel bluffs against stations, river bluff-raises, and bluff-catching large river bets and raises. Miller's rule for callers is "don't bluff them, but still barrel them, changing only which hands you bet" (marginal pairs instead of air) `[OPINION]` ([Miller, Card Player 2015](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66295-wynn-poker-room-28-3/articles/22241-four-skills-to-beat-2-5)). The c-bet size dispute (Fitzgerald: 66-75% pot on boards without two cards nine or higher; Upswing: about one-third pot because check-raises are rare and later streets over-fold) reflects different target opponents and should be resolved by each bot's fold-versus-size curve, not hard-coded ([Fitzgerald](https://jonathanlittlepoker.com/exploitativealex/); [Upswing 2019](https://upswingpoker.com/crush-live-poker-games-exploitative-tactics/)).

### The most expensive leaks of developing players

**No source ranks leaks by bb/100 or dollars per hour; any numeric cost ranking would be invented.** The only cost-like figures are Selbrede's modelled preflop EVs above (about $1 per instance for over-limping 22 on the button instead of raising). What exists is ordering and convergence. Miller orders his skills explicitly: (1) tight simple preflop play, (2) do not pay people off — "When people make big bets and raises on the turn and river, they aren't bluffing ... If you should fold, you should fold. Every time." — (3) assess hand value correctly `[OPINION, explicitly ordered]` ([Miller 2015](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66294-cppt-foxwoods-28-2/articles/22227-three-skills-to-beat-1-2)). Fitzgerald labels prevalence rather than cost: calling too much, hero-calling rivers with mediocre two pair, sets or straights, never value-betting enough, never 3-betting beyond the top 5%, tilt and unfocused volume are all "common" `[OPINION]` ([Fitzgerald, Card Player 2025](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/sam-greenwood-shares-his-punt-of-the-day-strategy/articles/card-player-magazine-38-11-strategy-10-mistakes-low-stakes-players-fitzgerald)). Hanson's list is check-calling rivers instead of bet-folding and fearing a river raise that is almost never a bluff; Lee Jones's is limping weak hands, failing to raise limpers, and paying off limpers who hit; Little attributes most of the achievable $1/$2 edge to "avoiding poor stack-offs and stealing pots when no one is interested" `[OPINION]` ([Little](https://jonathanlittlepoker.com/cashstrategies/)).

The research team's synthesis of those lists, ranked by how many independent authors name the leak and by pot size at the decision, not by any sourced cost, is: (1) paying off big turn and river bets and raises with one pair or bluff-catchers; (2) too many hands preflop, including open-limps, limp-calls, loose cold-calls and blind defence against large raises in raked pots; (3) missed and under-sized value, including checking back rivers and check-calling instead of bet-folding; (4) multiway errors, namely overvaluing top pair, c-betting air into three or more players and calling multiway raises; (5) bluffing stations; (6) flop over-commitment with vulnerable one-pair hands at medium SPR; (7) not isolating limpers or under-sizing so every pot goes multiway; (8) calling 3-bets too wide and not folding to tight players' 3-bets and limp-reraises; (9) over-folding to small bets and failing to stab at checked-through pots, a leak of tightened-up regulars; (10) slow-playing against callers; (11) applying solver concepts blindly; (12) tilt and fatigue. The convergence on the first three across authors with different commercial interests is the strongest signal in this research despite all being opinion. The four highest-confidence mistake flags are: bluffing a station; calling a turn or river raise from a passive player with one pair; limping behind instead of isolating a limper in position; and small value bets against a station.

## A5. Game conditions: $2/$3 and $3/$5 are California fixed-drop games; rake costs roughly 7-14bb/100; 5-10bb/hour is an opinion benchmark

No source enumerating every US room that spreads these blinds was reachable. The exact pot thresholds at which California rooms take each dollar of the drop were not found, which matters because a four-way limped $2/$3 pot of $12 would lose more than half its value if the full drop were taken at that size `[CALC, needs verification]`.

Cost per player is measured in one place only. In Selbrede's sample of nearly 1,000 Las Vegas hands (71% raked, 8.78 players, about 40 hands/hour), the average rake per hand dealt and per player-hour was $2.16 and $9.86 at $1/$2 with a $4 cap, $2.46 and $11.22 with a $5 cap (modelled), $2.72 and $12.40 with a $6 cap (modelled), and $2.92 and $13.99 at $2/$5 with a $4 cap; the average $1/$2 player loses "about 5 BB/hour" to rake against about 2.8bb/hour at $2/$5; a $1 jackpot drop adds about $3.24/hour `[DATA-live, small sample, 2019; $5 and $6 caps modelled]` ([Selbrede 2019](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)). Dividing by players `[CALC]`: about 14bb/100 at $1/$2 with a $5 cap and about 7bb/100 at $2/$5 with a $4 cap, as pool averages. Rescaled to 30 hands/hour the hourly figures fall by a quarter. No measurement exists for $1/$3, $2/$3, $3/$5, any cap above $4 at $2/$5, or any fixed-drop game; applying $6 + $1 to 71% of 30 hands among nine players gives about $16.50/hour, or 5.5bb/hour at $2/$3 and 3.3bb/hour at $3/$5 `[CALC on carried-over assumptions]`. Tips are opinion only and the estimates conflict: $8-$12/hour ([PokerCharts](https://pokercharts.com/en/blog/how-much-do-1-2-and-2-5-poker-players-make)) against the $4.50/hour implied by Selbrede's observed 4.56 pots won per player-hour at $1 per pot.

For EV scoring, three structural points follow from the schedules `[CALC/inference]`. Under 10%-capped rake every pot below $50-$70 is taxed at the full 10% plus the drop, so limped, single-raised heads-up and blind-versus-blind pots are the most distorted; a worked example is a $34 pot paying $3 rake plus $1 drop, an 11.8% tax `[OPINION/arithmetic]` ([SplitSuit](https://www.splitsuit.com/live-poker-cash-game-truths)). No-flop-no-drop creates a discrete EV step at the flop, so rake should be applied only on flop-seen branches except in fixed-drop rooms with a no-flop charge. And the solver baseline itself must be a raked, no-ante cash solution, or blind-defence flags will be wrong before any pool exploit is applied. No published solver analysis uses a live structure (a 1-2.5bb cap nine-handed, or a fixed drop).

### Pace, straddles and differences between stake levels

Hands per hour: about 40 with an auto-shuffler in the one observed sample `[DATA-live]` ([Selbrede 2019](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)); 25-30 or 20-30 in most opinion sources `[OPINION]` ([SplitSuit](https://www.splitsuit.com/live-poker-cash-game-truths)); 20-25 in Hand2Note's streamed games. A default of 30 with a 25-40 range covers every source. Straddle rules are set room by room and no frequency data exists for casino $1/$2-$3/$5; the 85% figure is for streamed $2/$5-$5/$10, and one forum poster reports a Mississippi-straddle room where "the button straddled 95% of hands" `[OPINION, forum]` ([PokerChipForum](https://pokerchipforum.com/threads/scared-to-take-the-leap-starting-1-3.129852)).

Quantified differences between stake levels are almost entirely economic: rake of about 5bb/hour at $1/$2 against 2.8bb/hour at $2/$5, and average pots of 25bb against 31bb `[DATA-live]` ([Selbrede 2019](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-impact-of-rake-in-low-stakes-cash-games-33298.htm)). Differences in player mix are asserted qualitatively and inconsistently: Selbrede says Vegas $2/$5 is "similarly loose" to $1/$2 `[DATA-live, no table accessed]`; Miller says pros and wannabes concentrate at $2/$5 and above and that $2/$5 players are more solid and so more predictably under-bluffed `[OPINION]` ([Miller 2018](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66390-george-antonakos-wins-cppt-bike-main-event-31-18/articles/23459-bluff-catching-at-1-2)); forum posters variously report no change between $1/$3 and $2/$5 or that $1/$2 players call large 3-bets that $2/$5 players fold `[ANECDOTE]` (snippet) ([TwoPlusTwo](https://forumserver.twoplustwo.com/170/live-no-limit-holdem-cash/1-2-vs-1-3-2-5-vs-5-5-whats-diff-1378241/)). No tracked VPIP, PFR, 3-bet or open-size data by stake was found, and nothing at all specific to $2/$3 or $3/$5 pools. Treating "$2/$3 plays like $1/$3" and "$3/$5 like $2/$5" for tendencies is reasonable only if stack depth and rake are set from the California parameters (research-team reading).

### Achievable win rates

**There is no public large-sample tracked dataset of live win rates; every benchmark is opinion or a small self-report.** The opinions converge: good 5bb/hour, strong 7-8, elite 10+ at both $1/$2 and $2/$5, described by the publisher as "industry consensus", not its own user data `[OPINION]` ([PokerCharts](https://pokercharts.com/en/blog/win-rate-benchmarks-live-poker)); "top-side" $20/hour at $1/$2 (10bb) and $40/hour at $2/$5 (8bb) `[OPINION]` ([SplitSuit](https://www.splitsuit.com/live-poker-cash-game-truths)); about 10bb/hour for the very best, which the citing author calls "wild speculations" `[OPINION]` ([TableLab](https://tablelab.app/blog/good-live-poker-win-rate)). The same publisher's other page is more generous ($1/$2 solid winners 5-10, exceptional 12-15bb/hour), and skeptics hold that 10bb/hour sustained at $2/$5 is "unheard of" (snippet) ([BlackRain79](https://www.blackrain79.com/2018/06/good-poker-hourly-rate.html)). Self-reports: a Las Vegas $2/$5 pro at $60 then about $50/hour (12 then 10bb/hour), sample not stated `[ANECDOTE]` ([Laplante, Card Player 2019](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66406-ocean-s-eleven-casino-32-7/articles/23641-poker-fundamentals-being-a-poker-pro-part-2)); Marc Goone's 300 hours at Hustler $5/$5 ($1,000 cap) at $89.61/hour, about 18bb/hour, which the article itself calls "not conclusive" `[DATA, single player, small]` ([Pokerati 2025](https://pokerati.com/2025/01/is-live-poker-still-beatable-in-2025-here-is-the-truth/)). Hand2Note's winners average 51bb/100, with 95bb/100 at $2/$5 over 52k hands, but with rake ignored, 500-1000bb stacks, 85% straddled and players selected for winning TAG stats; it is an upper bound on exploitability, not a casino benchmark `[DATA-live, other pool]` ([Hand2Note](https://hand2note.com/Blog/Databases/online-vs-live-poker-insights)).

Conversions at 30 hands/hour `[CALC]`: 5bb/hour is 16.7bb/100, 8 is 26.7, 10 is 33.3; in dollars 5-10bb/hour is $15-$30 at $2/$3 and $25-$50 at $3/$5. Variance is also opinion: standard deviation "commonly cited" as 80-100bb/100 `[OPINION]` ([PokerCharts](https://pokercharts.com/en/blog/how-many-hours-until-you-can-trust-your-win-rate)), which implies about 44-55bb/hour; with a true 5bb/hour rate and 50bb/hour deviation, the 95% interval after 500 hours is roughly 0.6-9.4bb/hour `[CALC]`, so an 18bb/hour result over 300 hours cannot distinguish elite from good-plus-variance. The share of long-term winners is given as 5% in one source and about 30% in others (one resting on a 609-player online sample); the figures are irreconcilable and none is live-measured ([PokerCharts](https://pokercharts.com/en/blog/win-rate-benchmarks-live-poker); [BlackRain79](https://www.blackrain79.com/2019/09/what-percentage-of-poker-players-are.html)). Trend claims (rake caps up from a $4 Las Vegas base case in 2019 to $5-$7 now; games "harder than in 2017 but still good") are secondary or opinion; benchmarks formed before about 2020 should be discounted.

## C. Fifty-one "solver says X, but against this pool do Y" spots

Each entry gives situation, opponent, the solver baseline, the recommended play, the reason, and the source with its evidence type. **Baseline: source-stated** means the equilibrium play is given in the cited source. **Baseline: researcher's characterisation** means the research team described what an equilibrium strategy typically does; it was not solver-verified and must be checked in the simulator's own solver before the spot is used as a test case. Many of the best-specified hands were played at $2/$5, $5/$5 or $5/$10 or come from tournament or online solver set-ups; these are flagged. Entries marked DISPUTED carry both positions. **Folding to aggression**

1. **Two pair facing a river shove over hero's own overbet.** Situation: $5/$10 (above target stakes), about $1,150 effective; hero K♠8♠ isolates to $40, button min-3-bets to $80, hero calls; Q♠6♣8♣ check-check; 4♠ hero bets $100, called; K♥ hero bets $450 into $380, button shoves; hero gets about 3.5-to-1. Opponent: older recreational player. Baseline: call, needing about 22% (researcher's characterisation). Play: fold unless the line cannot represent value. Why: late-street raises at low and mid stakes are almost never bluffs; hero called and lost to QQ. Source: Hanson, [Crush Live Poker](https://crushlivepoker.com/articles/raises-to-overbets). `[OPINION, one anecdote]`. Hanson still calls when the line makes no sense.

2. **Overpair on a flush-completing river: bet small and fold to a raise.** Situation: $1/$2, $300 effective; hero raises A♣A♥ to $10, button calls; K♣5♦2♣ bet $20, call; T♠ bet $50, call; river 7♣. Opponent: average low-stakes player. Baseline: mostly check, with check-call a large part of the response (researcher's characterisation). Play: small value bet, fold to a raise; check-call is ranked worst. Why: the pool checks back KQ/KJ, calls small bets with one pair, raises only with the flush, and under-bluffs when checked to. Source: Hanson, [Crush Live Poker](https://crushlivepoker.com/articles/rarely-check-call-the-river). `[OPINION]`.

3. **Top pair weak kicker facing check-raise flop, pot turn, jam river.** Situation: 100bb, 8-handed; hero button Q♥8♥ opens, BB calls; Q♣6♦4♥ hero bets small, BB check-raises to 7.2bb, call; 9♠ BB pots, call; 2♠ BB jams into 62.7bb. Opponent: straightforward or tight player. Baseline: flop call and turn call "the vast majority of the time" are source-stated (Q8 has about 47% against the raising range; solver re-raises about 3.6%); river frequency is not stated (researcher's characterisation: calls at some frequency needing about one-third). Play: fold river; folding the turn is reasonable against a tight player. Why: most players do not jam rivers as pure bluffs. Source: Little, [PokerCoaching 2026](https://pokercoaching.com/blog/top-pair-facing-check-raise-q8s-btn-vs-bb-100bb-cash/). `[OPINION]` for the exploit.

4. **Top pair good kicker facing small-then-big sizing.** Situation: $2/$5 (flag), $1,000 stacks; hero raised K♠Q♠; K♣8♦7♦ villain bets $60 into $82, call; 8♥ villain bets $80 into $202; 5♠ villain bets $250 into $362. Opponent: typical live $2/$5 player. Baseline: call (researcher's characterisation). Play: fold; do not call this pattern at worse than about 3-to-1; bets over $100 are "serious". Why: a small bet followed by a much larger one is a strong value pattern. Source: Miller, [Card Player 2016](https://cardplayer.com/poker-news/20644-poker-strategy-with-ed-miller-three-bet-sizing-tells). `[OPINION]`.

5. **Top pair marginal kicker facing a triple barrel from a nit.** Situation: hero J♥T♥ calls a raise; J♣5♦2♠-type board; villain bets three streets. Why: these players rarely triple-barrel bluff. Baseline: AQ mostly checks; sizes polarised between 25% and 150% pot (source-stated). Play: AQ mostly bets 40% pot; AJ and some KJ block-bet; sizes cluster at 40% and 100%. DISPUTED.** Situation: e.g. hero A♠2♠ on Q♠9♣5♣T♠K♠, bet about 2x pot; or AJ on A-9-8 with all draws missed, about 1.5x against a "non-believer". Opponent: recreational hero-callers; stations. 27. **Sizing against an inelastic caller.** Situation: BB versus cutoff, 60bb (tournament-style depth, flag), 9♥6♦4♣. Opponent: (a) BB never folds to a 33% bet; (b) BB calls any pair or ace-high at any size. Baseline: cutoff mixes checks and mostly large bets, EV about 3.75bb (source-stated). Play: (a) bet 33% with the whole range, EV about 4.71bb; (b) same frequency, overbets only. 28. **Fast-play sets three-way when a sticky player is behind.** Situation: cutoff c-bets small into button and BB on T♠7♥4♠; hero is the button. Opponent: BB locked to never fold top pair, open-enders or flush draws. Baseline: button mostly slow-plays 77 and 44, raises about 13% (source-stated). Play: raise 77/44 and more AT/KT/QT, about 19% overall. Why: raises get called by worse; the intuitive "raise less against sticky players" was tested and rejected. Source: Brokos, [GTO Wizard 2026](https://blog.gtowizard.com/3-way-how-to-exploit-a-calling-station/). `[SOLVER-SIM]`.

29. **Bet the overpair in a 3-bet pot instead of checking to induce.** Situation: SB 3-bet, button called, 7♣5♦2♥, hero AA. Opponent: passive in-position player who stabs too little. Baseline: SB checks much of its range including some overpairs; button stabs about two-thirds of range for 25% pot (source-stated). Play: bet. Why: cutting the stab frequency by 5% raises overpair betting by almost 20%. Source: Vanja, [GTO Wizard 2025](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the_3_biggest_leaks_killing_your_winrate/). `[SOLVER-SIM]`.

30. **Overpair multiway on a wet board: size up every street.** Situation: $1/$2, $100 effective (short); five limpers, hero SB Q♥Q♦ raises to $10, three see 9♣7♠5♥ (pot $47), hero bets $20, two call; 2♠ hero bets $25 into $107; A♦ river. Opponent: loose limpers. Baseline: multiway, smaller bets and more checking with one pair (principle source-stated, [GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/10-tips-multiway-pots-in-poker/)). Play: about $18 preflop, $32 on the flop, shove the turn; check the river and ideally fold to the shove. Source: Little, [Card Player 2019](https://cardplayer.com/poker-news/24134-poker-strategy-with-jonathan-little-betting-too-small). `[OPINION]`.

Opponent: recreational players who bet good, medium and drawing hands when checked to. Baseline: c-bets a share of range out of position (researcher's characterisation). Play: check nearly always, check-raise almost any hand with equity against a small stab, bet turn and river for value after a check-through. Opposite of spot 29; the deciding variable is stab frequency. Source: Goone, [Card Player 2024](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66553-tyler-ninja-blevins-37-23/articles/25224-marc-goone-explains-how-to-properly-size-your-bets). `[DATA-live, author-reported]`.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the build more than it fills in parameters. The premise that realistic low-stakes opponents can be parameterised from published numbers holds for one slice only: a loose-passive preflop model anchored on a single small, old, Las Vegas $1/$2 sample. Everything a postflop EV engine needs most (how often each type folds to each size on each street, how often a bet or raise is a bluff) is unmeasured, so the simulator's verdicts will be consequences of its own assumptions. The honest design is to make those assumptions first-class: a short list of explicit, tunable opponent parameters with the sourced direction as the default sign and no claim of empirical magnitude, and mistake flags worded as "an error against an opponent who does X" rather than as absolute.

The disputes among coaches turn out to be a specification. Each one resolves into a second-order parameter that separates opponents the archetype labels lump together: size-to-strength correlation, bluff frequency by line rather than globally, fold-versus-size elasticity (true station versus elastic recreational), slow-play frequency, stab frequency when checked to, fold-to-3-bet, and check-raise frequency. A bot defined only by VPIP, PFR and an aggression number will reproduce one side of each dispute and flag the other side as a mistake. Two further implications follow from the game-conditions evidence. The solver baseline that deviations are measured against must itself be a raked, no-ante, full-ring cash solution, or correct blind folds will be scored as errors. And "$2/$3 and $3/$5" is a claim about a place as much as a stake: with fixed drops and 20-100bb caps, the Southern California games those blinds denote need their own rake model and stack preset, and the widely quoted 5-10bb/hour benchmark was not formed there. The highest-value next evidence would be a few thousand hand-recorded hands at the target stakes with showdowns, or access to Selbrede's second volume and the Hand2Note database, since either would replace the largest block of assumptions with measurements.
